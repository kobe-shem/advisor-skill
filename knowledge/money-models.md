# Money Models — $100M Money Models (distilled)

## What a money model is
A money model is a **deliberate sequence of offers** — what you sell, when, and in what order to
maximize lifetime customer value. Most businesses have a product; fewer have an offer; almost
none have a money model. The goal is to capture maximum value from every customer across the
journey *and* to return acquisition cash fast enough that the model finances its own growth.

> For the unit-economics math underneath this (LTGP/CAC, the 30-day payback test, automation-
> adjusted minimum ratios), see `ltgp-cac.md`. This file is the offer-sequence architecture.

## The architecture
**Entry offer (attraction / low-ticket / free):** the first exchange of value. Purpose: convert a
stranger into a buyer. The lower the barrier, the more volume; even a $1 purchase flips the
buyer-psychology switch. Variants: low-barrier offer, free trial, free-with-consumption, paid
mini-audit.

**Core offer (main product):** where the primary value lives — this is where the Grand Slam Offer
sits. It should be your clearest, strongest offer.

**Ascension offer (upsell / premium):** the next level for clients who got results and want more.
"What's the next step for someone the core offer already worked for?"

**Retention / continuity (recurring):** the monthly/annual product that creates predictable
revenue. This is where real wealth and margin live; design the ascension path so continuity is
the natural next step.

## Client-financed acquisition (CFA) — the core mechanic
The model is engineered so the cash collected from a new customer in the **first ~30 days**
covers (or exceeds) the cost of acquiring them — and ideally covers delivery too. When that's
true, acquisition pays for itself and you can spend as fast as you can deploy, rather than
budgeting from a fixed marketing pool.

**The illustrative pattern** (Hormozi's gym case, paraphrased): instead of a $21 low-ticket entry
that takes weeks to recoup, structure a higher upfront entry plus fast same-week upsells and an
early prepay, so blended cash within 30 days is large. The competitor who collects $21 in 30 days
cannot outspend the operator who collects ~$1,000 in 30 days, in the same attention auction.

**Levers that pull cash forward / raise per-customer value:**
- Higher upfront entry price.
- Fast upsells (within 48 hours of purchase).
- Cross-sells (a different complementary thing, not just a bigger version).
- Early prepay for a longer term (annual prepay).
- Financing / payment terms that front-load cash; "we don't start work until paid in full."
- Careful downsells to capture non-buyers without cannibalizing premium buyers.

## The 30-day payback rule
Limit the payback window to ~30 days for bootstrapped businesses: it's roughly the longest most
small businesses can float cash, and it matches the interest-free window on credit. **Lifetime
value does not save a business that runs out of cash before payback.** Judge a money model by how
fast cash returns, not only by theoretical LTV.

## Decision rules
- If the business needs a *fixed* marketing budget because every new customer starts cash-negative, the money model is broken — fix payback before scaling spend.
- If you want to outspend competitors, increase what you collect in the first 30 days (entry price + fast upsells + prepay), not your tolerance for losses.
- Put the Grand Slam Offer at the **core**, not the entry — the entry exists to create a buyer cheaply, the core to deliver value, continuity to compound it.
- Don't add an upsell/continuity tier that overloads delivery capacity — a money model that breaks fulfillment churns the customers it sells.
- Build the recurring tier deliberately; retainer/continuity is where margin and durability live.
