# Leads — $100M Leads (distilled)

## The lead hierarchy
- **Lead** = a person you can contact.
- **Engaged lead** = a person who showed interest *and* gave you contact info.
- The goal is always to manufacture **engaged leads**, not raw traffic.

Sequence of value creation: get leads → get engaged leads → convert to buyers → maximize buyer
lifetime value. Most operators jump straight to "convert" while selling to the wrong people.

## The Core Four — the only four ways to get leads
Every lead-gen tactic in history is a variant of one of these:

1. **Warm outreach** — contacting people who already know you.
   - Start here, always; every founder has an untapped warm network.
   - Text / DM / email / call existing connections. Highest conversion, zero cost, most under-used.

2. **Content** — publishing value that pulls leads to you.
   - Go where your buyers already are; teach what you know to demonstrate expertise.
   - Slowest to compound, longest-lasting payoff. One great piece generates leads for years.

3. **Cold outreach** — contacting strangers at scale.
   - Email / LinkedIn DM / cold call. Requires a strong lead magnet or irresistible opening offer.
   - Personalization beats volume, but volume still matters.

4. **Paid ads** — buying attention at scale.
   - Fastest to scale, most capital- and testing-intensive. ~$100/day minimum for real signal.
   - Rule: if it won't sell organically, ads only lose money faster — they accelerate the truth.

## The Rule of 100
Pick **one** method and do it 100 units/day for 100 straight days without changing it:
100 outreach touches, or 100 minutes of content, or $100/day of ad spend. Consistency produces leads.

**Order of operations:** warm → content → cold → paid. Don't jump to ads before warm outreach is exhausted.

## Lead magnets — the trojan horse
A lead magnet trades real value for contact info; it's the first impression of the business.
- It should feel worth **more** than the cost of the core offer (a $5k offer wants a magnet that feels like $1k+).
- Format value, high → low: free trial / sample of the real thing → software tool → checklist/playbook → mini-course (3–5 lessons) → webinar → template/swipe file → PDF/report.
- Naming: `[Specific Outcome] for [Specific Audience] in [Timeframe] without [Objection]`.

## Referrals & word of mouth
Highest-quality source, hardest to scale, most neglected. Customers refer when they got results
+ felt good about the service + know someone who needs it. Engineer them:
1. Deliver outcomes worth talking about (table stakes).
2. Ask at the peak of excitement (right after a win, not weeks later).
3. Make it frictionless ("is there one person who'd benefit? Can I reach out?").
4. Incentivize referrers with real value, not just thanks.

## Affiliates & partners — borrowed distribution
Fastest way to scale leads without owning the audience.
- Find people who already have your ideal customers as *their* audience.
- Make the commission enthusiastic, not an afterthought; treat top affiliates as partners.
- Give them done-for-you assets: emails, scripts, landing pages.
- Math: 10 affiliates × 5 leads/month = 50 pre-qualified leads at zero ad cost.

## Decision rules
- If lead flow is the binding constraint, run the **Rule of 100 on one Core-Four method** before adding channels — thrashing across channels produces nothing.
- If you "can't get leads," exhaust warm outreach and referrals before spending on ads.
- If a lead magnet isn't converting, it's not valuable enough — make it feel worth more than the paid offer.
- If you have results-getting clients and no referral engine, that's the cheapest leads you're leaving on the table — build the ask into delivery.
- Don't scale paid ads on an offer that doesn't already convert organically.
