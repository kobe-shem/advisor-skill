# Alex Becker — doctrine card

## Use this card for
Meta acquisition for **B2B, SaaS, info, and call-funnel businesses** — the low-volume,
profile-funnel account structure and, above all, **signal engineering**: feeding qualified /
high-value conversions back into Meta so the machine optimizes toward best customers instead of
any converter. This is the industry-calibration counterpart to Nathan Perdriau: NP is the deeper,
better-evidenced media-buying layer calibrated on ecommerce; Becker is calibrated on a B2B shape of
business (B2B offer, booked-call funnel, mid-five-figure monthly spend, long consideration cycle,
small TAM). NP is the more thought-out and better-explained corpus; Becker maps better onto a
high-ticket B2B call funnel — route by the account's industry.

NOT for offer construction, pricing, or guarantees (Cole Gordon / Hormozi). NOT for
creative-concept construction, awareness-stage craft, attribution *measurement* truth, or
finance-backed volume math — that depth is NP's. When the account in question is ecommerce/DTC,
load NP, not this card.

## Core doctrine (distilled, evergreen)

- **Signal engineering: the conversion you send Meta is a training signal, not attribution.**
  Meta's optimization goal and your business attribution are two different systems [04:21]. The
  operator's job is to do part of Meta's job for it: capture conversions Meta never saw (calls
  booked off-platform, assisted conversions last-clicked elsewhere, purchases 30–40 days later)
  and push them back to the originating ad set via offline conversions [04:11, 35:56]. The
  externally-tracked real outcome (a qualified call that happened) is the north star; Meta's
  native conversions are supporting signal [36:08].

- **Optimize toward the BEST customer, not any converter.** Front-load the signal with qualified
  and high-value people [36:20]: send only qualified calls (not booked calls), or only customers
  whose predicted 12-month value clears a threshold (e.g. >$500), as the conversion event
  [37:52]. Join Stripe/Shopify/CRM revenue back to first click and let Meta learn the profile of
  the high-LTV buyer [37:01, 37:19]. Requires real traffic volume; at low volume step down the
  ladder: show-ups → qualified calls → highest-revenue segment [39:22]. He attributes the big
  performance gap between accounts to this, more than to structure or creative [38:58].

- **One self-contained funnel per customer profile.** Not one giant CBO for every buyer type (a
  "dumpster-fire CBO" [27:17]) and not thousands of competing ads. Each distinct buyer profile
  (e.g. ecom operators vs info sellers vs SaaS vs agencies for Hyros) gets its own campaign with
  its own creative, own lander, own conversion signal [15:42, 16:14]. Start with 1–2 profiles;
  add the next profile/problem only after the current one is scaled [26:31].

- **Inside a profile funnel: ~5–8 ads sequenced as a funnel, not a pile.** The campaign IS a
  funnel: a problem-hook entry ad (plain UGC-style talking head is fine [18:33]) → mechanism /
  content ads → direct offer → client-proof ad → objection-handling ad [13:09, 21:33]. B2B
  buyers need a sequence of exposures; no single ad closes them [23:39]. The system's coherence
  outperforms any single home-run ad [13:54]; a funnel ~70% right does most of the job without
  constant ad replacement [12:56].

- **Low creative volume, sniper iteration.** Explicitly rejects volume-first creative strategy
  (1,000 AI ads, 10–20/week) for this business shape [01:27]: excess ads compete with each other
  and Meta can't learn how the pieces fit [10:00]. Find the strongest problem hook per profile
  (test ~3 hooks [19:00]), then improve the *presentation* of the proven pitch — same script,
  new setting/format/angle — rather than launching new concepts [05:06, 10:41]. Don't rotate a
  working entry ad out over presumed fatigue; new people still enter through it [15:02].

- **AI creative = presentation variants of a proven pitch.** Use AI video (his example: Seedance)
  to re-stage a converting ad — stage, podcast set, street interview, desert, costume — while
  preserving the pitch verbatim [31:56]. Judge variants on whether the format makes the known
  pitch more scroll-stopping, never on novelty [32:10]. Test variants against the original in a
  flexible-ad test and promote winners into the funnel gradually so the core system stays intact
  [32:55, 33:23]. He states he previously mass-produced AI ads optimizing on ROAS/view-throughs
  and now calls that wrong [33:31].

- **Judge the system, never the ad.** No creative decisions on isolated ad-level ROAS, especially
  last-click [04:39]; an entry ad can look terrible on ROAS while feeding every downstream
  conversion [10:05, 35:37]. Test by swapping small parts of a working system, not by injecting
  batches of unrelated ads [10:25].

- **Profile-matched landers beat pretty landers.** Each buyer profile gets its own landing page
  speaking to its problem (for ecom marketers: CPA) with matched testimonials [27:58, 28:09].
  Clear-and-ugly outconverts beautiful-and-vague [28:40]. AI makes per-profile (even
  per-prospect) lander customization cheap [29:07]. Mobile-optimized call-booking flow over
  generic forms [30:17].

- **Protect the learning phase.** Don't scale a new ad set before it accumulates its first
  ~20–30 conversions; keep the proven head ad set live and sharing conversion data because that
  history is the trained asset [33:49, 34:04].

## Decision rules
- If the business books calls → the conversion event is qualified calls (or show-ups, volume
  permitting), pushed via offline conversions/CAPI — never form-fills or page views.
- If Meta's numbers and the CRM disagree → don't argue attribution; engineer the signal: send the
  CRM truth back into Meta and optimize toward it.
- If cold performance plateaus on a B2B account → before adding creative volume, check profile
  segmentation (is every buyer type in one campaign?) and funnel completeness (does the campaign
  contain problem→mechanism→offer→proof→objection coverage?).
- If a winning ad is aging → make presentation variants of its pitch before writing new concepts;
  introduce winners gradually alongside the incumbent, never wholesale swaps.
- If LTV data exists → threshold it (high-value vs low-value) and send only high-value as the
  conversion; re-fire value signals as later purchases land.

## Conflicts with other advisors
- **Creative volume (vs Nathan Perdriau).** NP: volume is the lever, finance-backed (~1 net-new
  concept per $1k/mo); fix fatigue with more *different* concepts. AB: 5–8 coherent ads per
  profile, iterate presentation on winners, volume creates self-competition. Reconciliation is
  industry calibration, not contradiction — NP's numbers come from broad-TAM fast-cycle ecom;
  AB's from small-TAM long-cycle B2B call funnels, and NP himself concedes thin markets and
  banked-winner accounts run lower volume. **Route by the account's industry; for B2B call-funnel
  accounts, AB's calibration is the default prior, with NP as the measurement-truth and
  concept-craft layer underneath.** See `cross-advisor/conflict-map.md`.
- **Attribution (vs NP).** No conflict, different jobs: NP measures truth (acquisition MER,
  reconcile windows, measurement pyramid); AB engineers the training signal fed back in. Use NP
  to verify the numbers are real, AB to make Meta learn from them.
- **Structure.** AB's profile-funnel ≈ NP's concept (persona×angle×offer) ad-set segmentation —
  same segmentation instinct, different vocabulary. AB adds the intra-campaign ad *sequencing*
  NP doesn't prescribe.
