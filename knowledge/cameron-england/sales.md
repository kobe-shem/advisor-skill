# Cameron England — Sales pillar (distilled)

## Use this card for
Questions about converting a booked call into a closed agency client: the call structure (intro/setting call → demo/close), discovery and how to elicit pain, building the beliefs that pre-solve objections, the objection-handling process, pricing/commitment mechanics on the call, post-call follow-up and re-offers, qualifying/disqualifying leads, and how to hire/manage/coach a setter+closer team to a target close rate. Also owns the operator's internal game: projections, energy management, conviction.

Does NOT own: where the leads come from or lead quality/funnel mechanics (that's the **lead-gen** pillar — this card assumes a lead is already on the calendar). Does NOT own client onboarding/delivery after the sale (**client-success / fulfillment-svd**) or the call-center build-out for the *client's* leads (**call-center**). NEVER use this card for the user's own pricing/packaging/ICP/guarantee — those are mutable facts of their business, kept separate from doctrine.

## Core doctrine (distilled, evergreen)

- **Close rate is the single highest-leverage number in the business.** Output = Volume × Leverage; skill IS the leverage. Define it as closed deals ÷ *showed* calls (not ÷ offers — that flatters it). Benchmarks: ≥25% = floor/mediocre, 30% good, 35% great (free-trial offers should close far higher). Same funnel, only close rate changes: 15%→30% can add ~14 deals / ~$84k contracted / ~$42k cash on a $20k ad budget with zero extra calls. BUT keep it in proportion — a 30%→40% lift is ~10% upside while tripling ad spend is ~200%; dial in the call, but don't over-obsess on the script when volume is the bigger lever. [COURSE-MEDIUM]
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/A — "1.Sales - START HERE" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: _calls — "deeper discovery to improve close rates" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

- **Selling is serving — "transference of belief over a bridge of trust"** (credited to Cole Gordon). Prospect is on Hell Island (current pain) wanting Heaven Island (desired state); your service is the bridge, their objections are the sharks underneath. You can't transfer belief you don't have, so conviction is the prerequisite — when Cameron lacked product proof he transferred conviction from *himself* (pitched a $10k Google-Ads retainer not knowing what PPC was). People only buy to alleviate pain or channel an unfulfilled desire; keeping a qualified prospect in decision paralysis is the worst thing you can do for them. Reframe selling as *prescribing*, like a doctor. Stay in the dance: expect hard objections AND expect to close — "a tango on a balcony in Italy at 11pm," never a fight.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/B — "1. Sales state of mind" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **People buy to close the gap between current and desired state, driven by emotional needs.** They buy the destination and how it *feels* (the Hawaii trip, not the Boeing 737's turbofan), not the vehicle/features. Tony Robbins' 6 human needs drive every decision; **certainty** and **significance** dominate in business buyers — "more clients" really means certainty + significance + growth. Buy with emotion, justify with logic. A radical state change is one step toward killing the prospect's current identity, which resists death — that resistance is where objections come from; hold them to their higher self.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/B — "2. Sales first principles" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The 7 beliefs / objectionless-close framework (PDCDSSM).** A prospect needs 7 beliefs to buy, each of which *pre-solves* a specific objection if built in discovery: **P**ain, **D**oubt (can't do it alone + other options inferior), **C**ost of inaction, **D**esire, **S**upport (spouse/partner on board), **S**olution (yours uniquely different/superior), **M**oney. Build them via Socratic questioning so the prospect concludes it themselves (consistency bias) — they believe their own conclusions over your arguments ("everything from your mouth is shit; everything from the prospect's mouth is gold"). Cover all seven and the only objections left are money/fear. The two and only goals of any call: (1) find out if/how you can help, (2) eliminate every objection before the close — covering the seven does both. (Cameron's note-taking version of the checklist substitutes **T**rust for **S**olution: Pain·Doubt·Cost·Desire·Money·Support·Trust.)

  → Lesson: 3.Sales/D — "3. Discovery - Gap" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Be proactive, not reactive, on objections (the Scooby-Doo hallway).** The script is a hallway of doors; behind each is a monster (a belief). The door you DON'T open in discovery is the monster that bites you at the close — skip cost-of-inaction → timeline objection; skip support → partner objection. Pre-handle proactive objections (pain, doubt, cost, desire, solution, money) in discovery; only reactive ones (spouse, true logistics, fear) wait for the end. ~95% of "objection problems" are actually poor-discovery problems (Cameron has reviewed 1,000+ calls).
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/B — "9. Objections 101" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **80% of the sale is made in discovery (metal detector + shovel).** Treat the script like a metal detector swept across the beach — each question one sweep; when it "beeps" (a vague or emotionally-loaded answer = X marks the spot), drop the script, grab the shovel (probing questions: "why / how / what do you mean / tell me more"), and dig 3–5 layers to the pot of pain, then resume. A script is read word-for-word and triggers sales resistance; a framework is a guardrail (bowling bumpers) — drill the script until subconscious, then discard it and listen. 80/20 talk ratio: prospect 80%, you 20%; only questions, restatements, and belief-breaking stories/analogies should leave your mouth (credited to Hormozi).
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/B — "7. Discovery best practices" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Never assume — always ask, even when you know the answer.** It's 10–100x more powerful when the prospect says it (internal pressure + consistency bias). Forbid the word "assume." Don't coach during discovery (you give expertise away free and surface the conversation — it lands far harder after 20–30 min of digging), and don't use hype/coddling language ("that's awesome / pretty normal / you're doing great") — it alleviates pain and closes the gap you're widening (Cameron's own fat-kid-coddled-by-mum story). Be okay with silence — first to speak loses; after a question, shut up and let them fill the void.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/B — "7. Discovery best practices" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Quantify both states to make the gap real; force accurate numbers.** Use "specifically" / "exactly" and frame "last 90 days" or "last month specifically" — owners default to quoting their best-ever month out of ego. Never accept "we're profitable" (50k rev/5k profit vs 50k/25k changes the whole call); ask "is that normal for you?" in a concerned tone to expose a hidden consistency problem; don't buff their ego when numbers are good — make their numbers feel insignificant so the gap feels large. Internal pressure (their own reasons) beats external pressure (fake discounts) — "you can never have too much internal pressure." Handle Need buyers (away-from-pain, runway/survival) differently from Greed buyers (toward-growth, opportunity-cost/data); a $200k/mo owner working 80hrs can be a Need prospect — "when there isn't a need, there is always a greed."
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/D — "3. Discovery - Gap" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Cost of Inaction is the engine of urgency** — make doing nothing feel more painful than investing (the toothache that becomes unbearable). Every cost question is framed **Permission → Context → Question**, delivered slowly as if you're genuinely thinking it through (a scripted-sounding question gets a shallow answer). Four plays, escalating: (1) **Personal cost** (runway/burnout: "what's your runway until you run out of savings or burn out?" — when they say "two months," the think-about-it objection evaporates; for losing-money prospects be direct: "the elephant in the room is you're burning cash"); (2) **Logistical cost** (greed buyers — chunk down their funnel number by number, dramatize each leak, "fix this ONE thing → +$Z/mo, so you're leaving $Z on the table every month," and even if nothing else worked that one fix pays for the program); (3) **Best-interest cost** (entrepreneur frame: "what if the next 3 years are exactly like the last 3?"); (4) **Last-attempt cost** (double-permission, blunt: "I'm not inspired by your reasons… is this a pipe dream or what's really at stake?" — high-risk, forces a decision). Without an explicit cost of inaction you get "let me think about it."
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/D — "4. Cost of inaction" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Position yourself as new/different by exhausting what they've tried AND been pitched — the most critical phase, where you spend the majority of the call.** Extract two things: what they tried + why it failed, and what they were pitched + why they didn't go. Even "I haven't tried anything" gets a **double tie-down** ("over 5 years in business you've really done no marketing?… and this is the first call like this?" — people struggle to lie twice). Use **assumptive questions** that make a best-practice sound normal ("I assume they gave you a detailed report — cost per lead, show rate, ROI — just pull it up?"); when they can't, you've created a gap without insulting the old provider (doubles as a status tip-off). **Lean OUT to make them lean IN** ("if it's crushing, why not just double down?" / "the setup sounds good — what's the biggest reason you're looking elsewhere?"). Your solution must be the simultaneous *explanation for why everything else failed* AND why you're the safest/fastest/easiest bridge (Hormozi value equation). "New is better than better" — pitch the gap, not the sameness. The Box A vs Box B model: the moment they hear a familiar mechanism ("Facebook ads") they file you in Box A where the only axis is price; you reach Box B (value axis) by selling the gaps.

  → Lesson: 3.Sales/D — "3. Discovery - Gap" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The pitch is a 2–5 pillar bridge: simple, problem-tied, benefit-led** ("simplicity is the ultimate form of sophistication"; sweet spot 2–3 pillars, 4 ok, 5 pushing it). Skeleton: high-level promise (specific outcome in specific time) → the pillars → very short delivery explanation. Pillar 1 = quick win/foundation; middle = main problem-solver + unique mechanism (put proprietary-named pillars first); final = solves the problem they'll have *after* you solve the first (builds huge trust — they're not even aware of it yet). Intro each pillar by tying to a stated pain ("you know how you told me…" — the single most powerful phrase, consistency bias), out-tro with a *qualitative* tie-down ("what questions do you have?" not "make sense?") so they sell themselves in a sell/sell cycle. Make them aware a problem exists *before* presenting the pillar that solves it. Confused mind never buys. Two pillar formulas: **benefit-linking** (feature→logical→emotional benefit→tie-down, for unsophisticated/analytical) and **problem-linking** ("us vs them," for sophisticated/burned markets).

  → Lesson: 3.Sales/D — "6. Creating your pitch" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Pitch delivery — three methods; Miro is the default.** (1) **Presentation-less** (pure verbal) for highly emotional prospects (a structured deck kills the emotional/buying state), unsophisticated or phone-only niches — ~10–20% of calls; (2) **Slide deck** (pitch.com > Canva/PPT, minimal text, speaker-notes, treat as a mini objection-prevention VSL) for analytical/detail buyers; (3) **Miro board, drawn live** — Cameron's default majority of the time, because it reads as a custom system breakdown not a canned deck (stays out of Box A), allows annotating/cross-outs (statistically higher engagement) and skipping irrelevant parts. Advanced: a **live call-center demo** where a standby agent calls the prospect within minutes during the sales-team pillar (let them *experience* the product), and a **live money-leak/ROI calculator** for numbers-driven prospects. As founder, even if you sell well scriptless you're likely winning on authority, not skill — adopt a *framework* before hiring closers (scriptless doesn't clone).
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/D — "8. Pitch presentation methods" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Temperature-check before price; never pitch price until they're 100% sold on the product.** Stop screen-sharing for intimacy, ask the open "what are your thoughts on how we do things?", read **answer + tonality + body language** (all three). If uncertain, run the alignment bit ("last thing I want is someone one foot in, one foot out") + the **1–10 scale** with humorous anchors ("1 = wasted 30 min with this random British guy, 10 = exactly what I need to hit 100k"). At **10** → "so it's just the money then?"; **8–9** → "what's keeping you from 100%?" (usually "I don't know the price," which is fine); **7 or below** → do NOT move to price, eliminate the uncertainty first. A too-fast 10 with shaky body language gets a double-check ("when someone gives me a 10 that quick, something specific resonated — what?"). Three uncertainties to clear: right solution, right time, right person. "It's too expensive" is usually an *uncertainty* objection misread as financial. If price is contaminating the score, factor it out ("price aside, on the process specifically, 1–10?").
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/D — "9. Commitment and price" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Drop price casually, minimized, with two options, then shut up.** Pitch ad spend FIRST and separately, double-tie-down they understand it ("that's separate, goes to Facebook daily, we don't get a penny — you know how ad spend works, right?") because a confused mind won't buy. Call it "investment," never "price/cost." Drop the fee minimized ("the investment to get you to X is only [A] or [B]"), do NOT change tonality or over-explain (the nervous over-explainer talks themselves out of the sale), offer two payment options (the choice itself overcomes the objection; burned prospects pick smaller-upfront, cash-rich pick cheaper-total), ask which they lean toward, then go silent (first to speak loses; if >~1 min, "what are you thinking about?"). Pre-frame onboarding/next-steps *before* price so they aren't mulling price + logistics at once; lead with a quick-win ("fast cash play" bringing jobs in week one). **Collect live, never via a post-call link** — buying probability collapses once the emotional window closes; ease in with a small commitment ("welcome to the family — let me confirm the billing address") before the card number, screen-share while typing to build trust; if it fails, "happens all the time — another card?" Handle disguised **buying questions** (guarantee, references, creative, "results timeline", "other agencies in my area") as objections in disguise: acknowledge + diffuse ("good question — did you ask that for a reason?"), let them reveal the real concern, *then* solve. Hold the PIF — when asked "can I break it up?" answer flatly "yeah, it's paid upfront" and shut up.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/D — "9. Commitment and price" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **An objection is an unticked mental box / disempowering belief — it's a dance, not a fight.** You + prospect vs the objection (the shark in the canyon), never you vs the prospect; your job is to show the shark isn't real (or show a safe crossing if it is) — "we suffer more in imagination than reality" (Seneca). ~9 of 10 first objections are smokescreens ("it's not you, it's me" — people tell you what you want to hear). Universal first-objection flow: **Pace** (zero emotion — "yeah, no problem"; never rationalize or reach for a payment plan, stoicism is the opposite of what they expect and surfaces the truth faster) → **Tie down/isolate** ("objection aside, is there anything else stopping you from feeling less than 100% certain?") → **should-vs-how** frame (if no money/partner mentioned) OR **double tie-down** (if they did: "money aside, nothing keeps you from 100%?" — over-emphasize 100%; you can't lie twice in a row, truth leaks at the back end) → if uncertainty surfaces, acknowledge honesty, re-do the temp check, lean on consistency bias ("you told me you were a 9 because X — now you seem nowhere near that, what's really going on?") or lay out the **3 choices** (not sure it'll work / don't trust me / matter of making it work financially). Lawyer model: fact-find → poke holes → rest your case. Break beliefs with **stories and analogies, not logic** — drill industry-specific analogies until they surface automatically. Only *attack* an objection when the prospect has fully conceded to your authority.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/B — "9. Objections 101" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/E — "2. Pacing the first objection" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The 3 objection buckets: Uncertainty (handle first), Financial, Support.** Uncertainty = not 100% on solution/timing/company ("think about it", "too risky", "no guarantee", and usually "too expensive"); reframe with the 7-reframe toolkit (chunk up/down, identity comparison, truth hammer, definition frame, emotion-vs-data, interest-vs-desire, future pacing) — drilled until you weave several fluidly, not picked consciously. **Financial** — only after product buy-in: two valid reasons (genuinely can't afford, or doesn't see value); never volunteer plans, "yeah it's paid upfront" + silence. Open the wallet only after a *confirmed* true financial objection: position plans as rare, protect their cash flow as leadership, probe exact cash → credit access → recap; payment plans are **bespoke not menus**, always take the highest upfront they can survive on (target ≥40% upfront cash). Pre-planned **downsell ladder** (PIF → high-upfront split → 50/50 → monthly → discounted first month, never improvised; start at the highest tier they can afford). If they won't share finances, it's NOT financial — call it out (Truth Hammer). Disqualify floor ≈ can't muster $1,000. **Support** — split FYI/respect (push to close, definition-frame that dumping a 10k decision on an under-informed spouse in 5 min is the disrespectful move) vs permission (mini-discovery → deposit ladder → follow-up); prevent the permission type by getting all decision-makers present (pre-frame on the intro with the leadership-structure question and the "how does your spouse *feel* about you scaling?" question).
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/E — "4. Uncertainty" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/E — "3. Price financial" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/E — "5. Spouse partner" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Deposit vs delay; never defer uncertainty.** A **deposit** (start $1,000 → $500 → $250 floor; refundable lowers friction; the active commitment matters more than the amount) is for real *logistical* objections — close it by *telling not asking* ("here's what we'll do — we'll put a deposit down to set up your account so we hit day 1 running"), state the amount AFTER the benefit (equal-exchange principle: never take money without giving value + a logical reason). A **delay** (intentional 2nd call) is for relational/complex/multi-decision-maker situations. Uncertainty is never deposited or delayed — it must be handled. The "thought it was pay-on-results" objection is uncertainty in disguise (reframe: POR is what unconfident agencies do, it misaligns incentives toward quantity over revenue); if you run a POR ad you must have a real (even unsexy) corresponding offer and qualify hard.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/E — "7. Closing deposits and Booking FU's" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/E — "6. Thought it was pay on results" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Push hard for the 1-call close AND have dialed follow-up — 30–40% of closes come from follow-up** (it moved Cameron's close rate from ~25–30% to ~35–40% all-time). Don't let a good follow-up system become an excuse to under-close ("don't act like a bitch on the call"); still kill smokescreens live (handle every objection you *can*; only true unhandleable logistics go to follow-up). After the door is truly closed for today, rebuild rapport 2–3 min so your energy doesn't curdle into "you're only worth money to me," then ALWAYS set the next step (**BAM-FAM — book a meeting from a meeting**) and add to the hot list.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/F — "1. Post demo follow up" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Follow-up wins because most people are inconsistent and generic — so be wildly consistent, personalized, and "selfishly selfless."** Never automate *post-demo* follow-up (pre-demo CAN be automated: hammer-them emails, objection-prevention videos, retargeting); tie it to that call's pain, answer "what's in it for them?" every touch, send as separate texts to look personal, connect on your personal cell at the end of the demo. Maintain a daily-non-negotiable **Hot List** (red-hot 24h–3d / hot ~7d / warm 7–30d / on-hold) and a weekly **Warm List** (30+ days, run via CRM tasks). Six message families: simple external-urgency (competitor / "someone else in [city] reached out"), founder-steps-in, value-driven (JusticeMap income map, mock campaign), personalized-funny, pain-driven, and the objection re-offer. Cadence: every 24–48h, daily/aggressive for 7–10 days on bullshit "think about it" stalls, then long-term nurture. Build **sales assets** (client-interview case studies per avatar, mock-up campaigns ~10–30 min for 60–80% prospects, objection-handling YouTube videos mined from your own calls, custom Looms) into one living doc; whenever an objection recurs, record an asset and put it on the thank-you page so it's pre-handled next time.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/F — "1. Post demo follow up" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/F — "4. Leveraging sales assets" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Re-offers liquidate the pipeline for an end-of-month cash injection** (~$10–30k/mo for Cameron; once ~$75k in the last 4 days of a month across two businesses). On the 25th, send a company-wide SOP to re-offer the *entire* pipeline (deposits, hot, long-term) — present a NEW offer that removes the exact reason they didn't close + scarcity + a hard deadline + a logical reason ("we want people in before the P&L closes"). Volume play: ~30–100 reached → 5–20 reply → calls → closes. Four script types (direct/indirect × scarcity), founder-voice variants ("I had a meeting with Cameron this morning, he wants to support you financially"). Phone first; if no pickup, a curiosity text pulls them onto the phone — **never explain or handle objections over text, the only goal is the call**. Expect less cash per deal; a close beats no close. Run reactivation 2×/month over no-bookers, non-closers, no-shows, 30+ day-old leads.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/F — "Re-offers" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Qualify ruthlessly but gracefully — unqualified prospects are "time thieves."** A *tough* prospect (has money, skeptical from past burns, follows the process) ≠ a genuinely *unqualified* one — never assume broke; get them to verbally admit "I cannot afford this" before you DQ. Use a green-flag/red-flag doc so the whole team qualifies to one standard. **Financial ladder**: soft qualifier first (always framed as INVESTMENT — "dollar in, five out" — never spend/cost; "how much have you set aside or could finance, if the ROI made sense?", read congruence across words/tonality/body-language) → hard price drop only on incongruent signals → financing bridge tied to *their outcome* (fund the 100k/mo, not paying you). DQ gracefully and "squeeze the juice" on a lead you paid to acquire: referral (with a fee), downsell to a lower-ticket product, long-term nurture, free resource — today's no is tomorrow's referral. "I only want to pay when I get paid" is a common objection in sophisticated markets, NOT a DQ. The lower your revenue the more you sell to everyone; the higher, the more you can tighten ICP.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/C — "6. Disqualifying and financial Q's" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Setting runs the pipeline like a nervous system; speed-to-lead is king.** Four-bucket Slack flow (new leads & texts / follow-up / call confirmations / email-opened); claim a lead with your emoji, open the script, and **double-dial back-to-back instantly** (call into voicemail then re-call to bypass Do-Not-Disturb) + text a 3-message sequence within 5 min; always call first even when they text you. MIT speed-to-lead: contact within 5–10 min ≈ 400% more likely to reach a meaningful conversation; minute 5→30 ≈ 100x drop in contact rate. ~11% connect on the 1st call vs ~90% by the 6th touchpoint; manually-confirmed calls show 80–90% vs unconfirmed 40–50% (manual phone confirmation can lift show rate ~30%+); the further out the booking, the lower the show. **Priority Action Chain** (drop down to it whenever a higher item appears, unless mid intro): intro calls → new leads + inbound texts → confirm next-day appts + push forward → hot-list → power-dialer (no-shows/nurture) → email-opens → reactivation → admin → training. Do admin/training only at end of day (low willpower); sell when willpower is high ("eat the frog"). Build a GHL power dialer past ~10–20 leads/day. Above all the **lion vs fat-cat** mindset: the money is in scrappy outbound, not the bare minimum of taking self-booked calls.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/C — "1. Setting process and pipeline management" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Pre-pitch creates the cliffhanger that drives demo show rate** (the Lindt free-chocolate-sample / Love-Island "next time" trailer). The intro is 90% discovery / 10% transition: collect non-emotional **ammunition** (load the SMG for the closer), find the X's but leave the *treasure* (deep emotion) for the demo — emotion dissipates over the 1–3 day gap. **5-step pre-pitch**: problem (mirror their exact situation) → why it's a problem → cost → unique mechanism (high level, outcome not tactic) → benefits, preceded by a permission question + a **status tip-off** (indirect bragging that builds authority without ego). Book the soonest slot (2-option close — "2pm or 4pm, which works?", like Visa-or-Mastercard, never "when works for you?"), pre-handle "let's book in a week" ("our system doesn't allow more than 48h out"), get them to manually click Yes on the calendar invite live, **double tie-down** ("short of getting hit by a bus, let me know if anything comes up — no-shows on [closer]'s calendar reflect poorly on me"), send homework matched to their concern. Demo show <70–80% on a 2-call process is a setter skill issue, not ads.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/C — "5. Transition to demo (pre-pitch)" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Always start 1-call; move to 2-call only when forced.** Less friction = more flow (one-call ~25% total no-show vs two-call ~40% collective). Default to a single call; split into setter→closer only for: (1) high volume + high DQ (>40%) — setter as gatekeeper; (2) consistent multiple decision-makers — pre-handle the partner objection on the intro (a scheduling-page qualifier alone won't work, "nobody read it"); (3) burnt/skeptical markets needing trust-building (send assets + hammer them) OR very unsophisticated markets (use the intro as an education call); (4) sophisticated/unemotional B2B (>$50–100k/mo, SaaS) — gather logical ammo on the intro. Never 2-call for emotional/impulse offers (dating/fitness/info), sub-$3k tickets, or before you've validated a strong 1-call close (≥25–30%). Closer using the setter's ammo: confirm only 2–3 high-level points (don't regurgitate everything — sounds scripted), use it as a launchpad to dig deeper for congruency, never re-ask raw questions.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/A — "4. 2 Call close adjustments" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Operator state and projections drive performance more than the script.** Run *projections* (calculated from inputs: calls × show rate × close rate × upfront-cash %), not woo-woo *goals* — set a **Blood Goal** (bare minimum the numbers say) and a **Stretch Goal** (~20–30% above, hit only 75–80% of the time, requires chasing the hot list / outbound / re-offers; if you hit it 100% of the time you're under-projecting). Every KPI needs a DRI; the rep who takes the calls sets their own projection (self-accountability), reviewed every Monday. Miss reasons: accuracy (projected best-case not historical), volume (not enough outbound), efficiency (skill gap/rut), or time (maxed-out → hire). Watch **leading indicators** (energy/biology/focus, graded 1–10 in EOD reports; <6–7 triggers an immediate 1:1) — they predict a close-rate dip before lagging numbers move. Manage energy across physical/emotional/mental; protect conviction with a **conviction bank** of wins (dips most in founders + high-churn months — a *shielded* closer keeps full conviction, which is one reason a separate salesperson can out-close the founder). A **rut** = a sustained ~5% close-rate drop over ~30 days (not one bad week — ignore weekly variance); most ruts come from ego/deviation from the process. For solo founders, ~50–90% of work time goes to sales.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/A — "2. Keys to high performance" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Expertise beats friendliness — adopt the doctor/strategist frame.** Rapport is built on competence, not commonalities (a broken-arm patient wants it fixed, not small talk) — diagnose, then prescribe; "whoever controls the framing controls the sale" (the one asking questions controls it). Given a choice, buyers pick the cold expert over the warm amateur (halo effect — looking clean/professional/branded implies world-class delivery; Cameron's closer jumped off ~10% by wearing a blazer + branded background). For sophisticated buyers who've seen 50 pitches, *be* the strategist who goes deep on their numbers, not a salesperson. Master via repetition (write the framework by hand 5–15×, drill out loud, embody it like Leonardo DiCaprio — no script in hand) and relentless call review (≥1–2 of your own + others' daily) — "repetition is the mother of skill; you need to be reminded more than taught."
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/A — "7. Sales success and common pitfalls" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/D — "2. Setting the frame" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Hire to solve a constraint, never "hire-happy"; closers eat what they kill.** A setter solves three constraints (lead-to-booking %, dollar-per-lead, closer efficiency — warranted once the closer spends >30% on unqualified leads, or ~150+ leads/mo at <50% booking, or even ~$100/day spend); a closer solves a founder-availability constraint (85–95% calendar capacity). Don't delegate sales if you can't do it (you can't train it or have validated the offer). Validate first: ≥25% close, ≥20–30 recorded closes, ~3 calls/day supply, fulfillment already delegated. **Rule of Two** (hire two closers at once); **coachability + will > experience** ("a ball of clay" — make a 6–7 a 10, not a 2 a 10); pure **commission on cash collected** with clawback, no base ("base breeds complacency"), OTE off historical not peak. Hire via group interview → 1:1 → mock-call (test coachability with harsh feedback + a re-run); source closers from paid low-ticket sales communities (the barrier filters quality). Shield the sales team from churn/refund news (separate sub-accounts, tiered announcements) to protect conviction.
  → Lesson: 3.Sales/A — "6. Hiring setters and closers" · [COURSE-HIGH]

## Decision rules

- If prospect gives a surface answer ("I just want more clients") → do NOT accept it; reflect + segment ("volume, quality, or consistency issue?") and dig with the shovel until you hit pain. · `lesson: 3.Sales/D — Discovery - Gap`
- If you hear emotional or vague language ("frustrating", "it's okay but…", "got burned") → drop the script, ask short open probes, compound 3–5 layers deep, then resume. · `lesson: 3.Sales/A — Sales success and common pitfalls`
- If a number question gets a rambling "it depends" dodge → tie back and re-ask ("for sure — so how many jobs?"); never move on without the number. · `lesson: 3.Sales/D — Discovery - Gap`
- If they "haven't tried anything" → run the double tie-down (ask twice, different ways, act surprised); if truly nothing AND first call ever → skip the doubt phase. · `lesson: 3.Sales/D — Discovery - Gap`
- If you're getting objections at the close → assume weak discovery first; check which of the 7 beliefs / which "Scooby-Doo door" you skipped — don't brute-force the objection. · `lesson: 3.Sales/B — Objections 101`
- If the prospect is struggling/emotional (need) → personal/runway cost; if successful/unemotional (greed) → logistical/opportunity-cost data + the "fix this one thing" frame; if you hit a wall → best-interest (entrepreneur) cost, then last-attempt cost. · `lesson: 3.Sales/D — Cost of inaction`
- If an objection appears → pace with zero emotion FIRST (no rationalizing, no payment plan), then tie down it's the *only* blocker, isolate "should I vs how can I", run a mini-discovery before answering. · `lesson: 3.Sales/E — Pacing the first objection`
- If you get a first objection → handle Uncertainty before Financial before Support; if "too expensive", confirm it's not actually uncertainty before offering any plan. · `lesson: 3.Sales/B — Objections 101`
- If they ask for payment plans → "yeah, it's paid upfront" + silence; never mention plans until a true financial objection is confirmed (open the wallet first). · `lesson: 3.Sales/E — Price financial`
- If "I can't afford it" → don't believe it until you've opened the wallet; if they won't share finances it's NOT financial — Truth Hammer. Known-wealthy prospect → skip the wallet, it's uncertainty. · `lesson: 3.Sales/E — Price financial`
- If a spouse/partner answer is ambiguous (respect vs permission) → assume permission (worst case); a non-financial-decision-maker only (office manager) → don't take the demo, except a very large multi-location company (known multi-call). · `lesson: 3.Sales/E — Spouse partner`
- If only a logistical objection remains (100% sold) → close a deposit (tell, don't ask; benefit then amount). Uncertainty → never deposit/delay, handle it. · `lesson: 3.Sales/E — Closing deposits`
- If on the temperature check they're at 7 or below → do NOT present price; surface and resolve what's keeping them from 8–10 first. A too-fast 10 with shaky body language → double-check. · `lesson: 3.Sales/D — Commitment and price`
- When dropping price → pitch ad spend first/separately, minimize ("only/just"), give two options, ask which they lean to, then stay silent; collect live, never a post-call link. · `lesson: 3.Sales/D — Commitment and price`
- If a buying question appears (guarantee/references/creative) → acknowledge + diffuse + "did you ask that for a reason?" and dig before answering. References → ask why; they're biased, the real want is "will it work for me." · `lesson: 3.Sales/D — Commitment and price`
- If a deal doesn't close on the call → rebuild rapport 2–3 min, BAM-FAM, add to Hot List, follow up every 24–48h (daily for 7–10 days on smokescreen stalls), then long-term nurture. Pre-demo follow-up CAN be automated; post-demo MUST be personal and done by the caller. · `lesson: 3.Sales/F — Post demo follow up`
- If a 60–80% prospect has one logistical blocker → build a mock-up campaign (reciprocity); don't waste team time on ~50% leads. · `lesson: 3.Sales/F — Leveraging sales assets`
- If it's end of month with a warm-but-cold pipeline → run a re-offer (new offer removing their exact blocker + scarcity + deadline + logical reason); never reveal terms over text, get them on the phone. · `lesson: 3.Sales/F — Re-offers`
- If you get financial red flags → soft qualifier first, hard price drop only if no straight answer, then financing tied to their outcome; DQ gracefully and squeeze the juice (referral fee, downsell, nurture). · `lesson: 3.Sales/C — Disqualifying and financial Q's`
- If a fresh lead comes in → double-dial within minutes + text within 5; confirm every booked call and push it sooner; the booked intro time is just a placeholder, call now. Sub-70–80% demo show = setter skill problem. · `lesson: 3.Sales/C — Setting process and pipeline management`
- If lead flow drops → a lion does 5x output (dials, outbound, power dialer, request reactivation); a setter who only works self-booked calls is a fat cat — reset expectations, coach up or out by ~week 4. · `lesson: 3.Sales/C — Setter common pitfalls`
- If pinned for price on the intro → deflect with an industry analogy ("custom, like a kitchen-reno quote with no photo"); if they still push, give only a wide open-ended RANGE — never lay out all the cards (kills the cliffhanger). · `lesson: 3.Sales/C — Setter common pitfalls`
- If you're launching/validating an offer → run 1-call; only move to 2-call once volume/DQ%, multiple decision-makers, burnt markets, or unemotional B2B force it. Don't change process after 3 bad calls (an "impulsive monkey"). · `lesson: 3.Sales/A — 2 Call close adjustments`
- If considering a discount → only with a genuine logical reason (calendar opening, case-study cohort, waived setup fee for acting today); arbitrary "fast-action" discounts reek of desperation and never go on the deck. · `lesson: 3.Sales/A — Sales success and common pitfalls`
- If you're missing projections → diagnose accuracy vs volume vs efficiency; if accuracy/efficiency are fine for a solo founder, it's a time constraint → hire a setter or closer. · `lesson: 3.Sales/A — Keys to high performance`
- If a prospect rambles → pre-frame early ("ok if I interject to keep us on track?") and ask for answers "in 1–2 sentences / just the number"; interrupting is fine — they get interrupted all day. · `lesson: 3.Sales/A — Sales success and common pitfalls`
- If a prospect is blunt/D-type ("just tell me the cost") → reciprocate the energy, use the direct frame + tough-love reframe ("cool if we cut the bullshit?") — these close fastest. If S-type → slow/methodical/reassuring; if C-type → mechanism detail + numbers + due-diligence assets. · `lesson: 3.Sales/C — Collecting ammo (Discover)`
- If a rep wants to DQ a borderline lead instead of pitching → default to selling and refund if needed (forgiveness > permission). Track close rate on LIVE calls not "qualified" calls, so closers can't cherry-pick. · `lesson: 3.Sales/A — Sales success and common pitfalls`
- If hiring a closer → Rule of Two; commission-only on cash collected with clawback; no base; OTE off historical not peak. Can't sell your own offer at ≥25% / 20–30 recorded closes → don't hire (unvalidated). · `lesson: 3.Sales/A — Hiring setters and closers`
