# Cameron England — Service Delivery (fulfillment-svd) pillar (distilled)

## Use this card for
Questions about **delivering the result after the sale**: how to build a fulfillment machine that gets predictable client outcomes, retain clients and raise LTV, structure onboarding/launch, map a client journey, diagnose-and-fix the throughput constraint, and staff/cost the delivery team as you scale (COGS, pod economics, capacity, when-to-hire).

Owns: service productization, the client journey + milestone/RYG scorecard, onboarding & launch SOPs, fulfillment economics (COGS, pods, capacity), the DIY/DWY/DFY model spectrum, and constraint diagnosis across the leads→bookings→shows→closes funnel *from the agency's delivery seat*.

Does NOT own: writing/scaling the actual ads (that's the **lead-gen** pillar), how leads get nurtured into booked calls (**call-center** / nurture pillar), or closing the prospect on the agency itself (**sales** pillar). The constraint *framework* lives here; the per-pillar fixes live in those siblings.
Never use this card for the user's own pricing/packaging/ICP/guarantee — those are mutable facts of their business, kept separate from doctrine.

## Core doctrine (distilled, evergreen)

### Retention & the economics of keeping clients
- **Two laws of a real agency: sign more than you lose, and make more from who you have.** Three growth levers — sign more, cut churn, raise what each client pays in the same period. Cutting churn is the keystone: it immediately opens margin AND raises the hard cap on how many clients you can carry. Anyone can hit one $100k month with a $30k ad burn + crazy guarantee; sustained six-figs/mo needs a predictable acquisition channel *and* retention (churn ideally sub-12%). Prefer 5 clients at $5k for a year over 30 churning monthly.
  → Lesson: 7.SVD/Fundamentals — "1.Start here" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Churn-cap math (why churn caps headcount):** 50 clients, signing 10/mo at 20% churn → stuck at 50; drop churn to 10% at the same sign rate → cap rises to ~90+. Signing harder without fixing churn just raises churn in equilibrium and you stay capped.
  → Lesson: "1.Start here" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Retention is won on two pillars: Results and Relationship.** If one is weak the client depends entirely on the other — strong relationship buys patience when results dip; great results survive a rough relationship. SVD trains the Results pillar (Relationship is the Client Success modules). "Product is king": you can outspend competitors on CAC if your retention/economics are better, and a great product compounds word-of-mouth.
  → Lesson: "1.Start here" · [COURSE-HIGH] · reinforced [COURSE-MEDIUM] (Founder call "Fulfillment, retention and lifetime value")
- **Sell on CAC-to-LTGP, not front-end ROAS.** A client who looks bottom-20% on a 30-day snapshot can be top-20% over 12 months (gyms, real estate, memberships). Educate the math AND pull cash forward (annual plans, paid-in-full, financing) so you're cash-profitable inside 30 days. "You can't ask someone to marry you on the first date" — use a low-barrier "bait" front-end offer that covers cost-per-show, then ascend the value ladder. Worked CAC example: $5k spend ÷ 10 clients = $500 CAC; ($5k LTV − $1k fulfill) ÷ $500 = 1:8 LTGP:CAC.
  → Lesson: 7.SVD/Fundamentals — "3.60 20 20 rule" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Three ways to raise LTV:** charge more, 80/20 onto your top clients, and add value then raise price (e.g. add a sales coach + AI design tool, then bump the retainer).
  → Founder call: "Fulfillment, retention and lifetime value" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]
- **"Dangle the carrot" + never lose the illusion of control.** Always have something new/exciting to show clients. Even when the market is genuinely terrible, telling the client you've lost control destroys their hope and they churn the moment they sense it. Always give the client a LEVER ("here's what we'll do"), never "it's all on you."
  → Founder call: Marbella Q4'24 Mastermind Session 2.0 · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

### The 60/20/20 distribution
- **The 60/20/20 rule of client results.** ~20% get incredible results (10–50x, shout your name), ~60% mediocre (just hit the outcome, profitable), ~20% fail (break even/lose, churn in 30–90 days). It's an internal average that varies by niche — **never disclosed to clients**; the less of fulfillment you control, the bigger the bottom 20%. Accept ~20–30% just won't get results; plan and build culture around it rather than chasing 0% failure (ego, hurts growth). Don't turn off acquisition or swarm the whole team onto one struggling client.
  → Lesson: "3.60 20 20 rule" · [COURSE-HIGH] · reinforced [COURSE-MEDIUM] (Marbella Session 2.0)
- **Three causes of the distribution: client-side, market-side, agency-side.** Client-side (poor execution / unrealistic expectations) is fixable via expectations; market-side (saturation/economy/local radius) is outside control; agency-side (wrong offer/strategy/team) is fully in your control = easiest to solve. It's usually a mix. Stories: *New Use Bars* (top-20%) made ~$100k new cash in first 30 days on a $5k budget with nothing done differently — right place/time/market. *Thunder Bay vs Miami* — a promo that crushes in a low-competition town flops in a saturated one. *The big franchise burning $50 front-end* (a top-3 US med-spa chain at ~$60k/mo runs a $50 intro that costs ~$100 because LTV is huge) prices small competitors out — they must run ~$300 offers.
  → Lesson: "3.60 20 20 rule" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Shift the ratio from both ends.** Filter high-risk clients before signing — the two red flags are budget scarcity (~$1k to their name → emotional, expects results in a week) and being a pain *before* paying. Build a **Perfect Client Traits doc** (~15–20 non-negotiable + nice-to-have traits derived from your top 20%, held by both Sales and CS) to filter and to retarget. Run a ~30-day rescue on the bottom then cut cleanly (keeping bad-fit clients poisons culture/team morale/reputation). **Analyze + clone the top 20%:** find common traits, retarget messaging, and feed those clients back to the pixel as the conversion event to find more like them.
  → Lesson: "3.60 20 20 rule" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Undersell and overdeliver — never create a negative expectation gap.** If you can deliver in 7 days, say 2–4 weeks. A good outcome delivered later than an oversold promise still reads as failure. Tell clients what they NEED to hear, not what they want — story: a client made $20k on a $3k budget then wanted to quit to chase a personal brand; Cameron told her it was "the stupidest thing you could say — repeat successful actions"; she left frustrated, then texted back and stayed.
  → Lesson: "3.60 20 20 rule" · [COURSE-HIGH]

### Clients buy ROI — fulfillment is a throughput game
- **Clients only buy ROI; fulfillment is throughput across four buckets: Leads → Bookings → Shows → Closes.** Internally CPL/cost-per-booking/show/close matter; the client only sees the return (pay $8k for 90 days, make $80–100k back). The deeper down the funnel, the less control you have — full control over leads, half over bookings/shows, least over closes (unless you close for them). Drop-off compounds: 100 leads → 50 book → 40 show → 10 qualified → 8 close. (Show-rate drops out for home-visit / B2B / virtual models — adapt to your model.)
  → Lesson: "1.Start here" · [COURSE-HIGH] · also "6.Client fulfillment overview"
- **The 4-pillar constraint tree is the diagnostic spine of the whole business.** Every client problem reduces to one pillar — not enough leads / leads not booking / booked not showing / shows not closing — and each pillar has a SOP'd lever set. People irrationally judge the whole system by the final outcome ("no money → it doesn't work"); usually three stages are fine and ONE is the leak, and most leaks trace back to the offer. Fix the one leak and the funnel runs.
  → Lesson: 7.SVD/Fundamentals — "Constraint diagnosis (CSM module)" · [COURSE-HIGH] · reinforced [COURSE-MEDIUM] (Founder call "constraint diagnosis framework")

### The client journey (flywheel)
- **Run a flywheel, not a funnel.** 5 phases (Awareness → Consideration → Purchase → Retention → Advocacy) loop: *successful* (not just happy) clients generate referral-partners/testimonials/case studies that feed Awareness for free and put acquisition on steroids. **Retention starts the moment they pay**, not months later.
  → Lesson: 7.SVD/Fundamentals — "4.Client journey overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Document the journey as a factory line (Pillars + Glue).** Legend: AP = activation point (client touchpoint); M = milestone (a benchmark, client need not be involved); Glue = day-to-day async comms (Slack/text/email) between calls; Aha moment = wonder + understanding that reinforces the buying decision; BamFam = Book A Meeting From A Meeting. Map the calls/events, define milestones **starting from the END and working backward**, give each a Red/Yellow/Green criterion graded on **leading indicators** (controllable, observable now — e.g. did they fill the stat form) not the lagging RYG result. Build Problem→Solution→difficulty tables per step so the team solves without escalating. Analogies: a gourmet-hot-dog / Tesla production line (2.3M cars/yr because everyone follows one A-to-Z process with an error logbook).
  → Lesson: "4.Client journey overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Cameron's six condensed milestones (med-spa example):** M1 Onboarding → M2 Launch → M3 First booking → M4 First showed patient → M5 First treatment-plan upsell / hit ROI projection → M6 Upsell additional ad spend → (final) upsell to the back-end 6-month program. More greens = higher probability of ascending/retaining. Analogy: 100kg→70kg weight loss is a chain of milestones each feeding motivation for the next (caloric intake = leading indicator, scale weight = lagging).
  → Lesson: "4.Client journey overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **The first 30–90 days is the High Churn Risk Zone** — buyer's remorse peaks; clients judge QUALITATIVE signals (professionalism, comms, added value) before quantitative results land. Front-load touchpoints: welcome call within ~1 hr, onboarding form before the onboarding call, ≥1 contact/day for the first 7 days, check-in #1 ~day 7 (+ onboarding NPS), check-in #2, then bi-weekly adaptive by client health.
  → Lesson: "4.Client journey overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **BAMFAM — never end a call without the next one booked.** Offer two specific times, not the option to not book. Activate **Advocacy when the iron is hot — at a WIN, not at churn** (hit ROI/M6, first upsell, record month). "Ask if they want dessert after the steak, not after they've left the restaurant."
  → Lesson: "4.Client journey overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Build the journey with AI:** fill the Client Journey Creation Worksheet → export PDF → feed an LLM (Cameron names Manus) your company-identity drive (market research, sales scripts, VSL/Fathom transcripts, example emails) so it embodies your voice → give it the worksheet + his example → have it output the journey as a Google Doc.
  → Lesson: "4.Client journey overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]

### The five foundations of client results
- **#1 Productize: "simple scales, fancy fails."** Get Client #1 the result the same way you get Client #1000 (iPhone analogy — same core build). Custom-per-client fulfillment is "where agencies go to die" — impossible to document/delegate, caps you at ~$20–40k/mo. Productize when retainers are sub-$5k/mo (~99% of agencies can); only go custom when each client is worth $20–50k/mo. Stories: Jiro (3 Michelin stars, one 20-piece menu, no customization); Subway (same process, few variables); Cameron's friend's info-product agency stuck below $30k/mo for years because every client's fulfillment differs.
  → Lesson: 7.SVD/Fundamentals — "5.Foundation of client results" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **#2 "Eat the client's complexity" to build a moat (castle metaphor).** Ads = wooden fence; + call center = stone wall; + sales training = drawbridge; + proprietary software/CRM/community = archers on the wall. You're paid in proportion to the complexity you absorb. Apple/iPod analogy: Apple ate ~99% of music's complexity (hardware + software + store) so the customer got the simplest experience — the more you absorb, the more dominant.
  → Lesson: "5.Foundation of client results" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **#3 Replicable delivery: one problem, one client type, one core offer.** Beware the "yes problem" (saying yes to SEO/social builds a Frankenstein business). Clients don't care *how* you get the result; the unique mechanism is marketing, but the core service stays simple. Michael-Jordan-can't-master-three-sports: energy is a laser, split it and you progress less everywhere. Only add product lines after $100k/mo, and prefer white-labeling.
  → Lesson: "5.Foundation of client results" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **#4 Condense Time To Value — first booked appointments within ~7 days of launch.** Two day-one levers: **Database Reactivation** ("fast cash play" — pull the client's existing list into the CRM, text+email blast, call responders, book them) and **immediate training access** for intangible value (packaging, selling, objections) before paid ads land. Fast first win ≈ long retention.
  → Lesson: "5.Foundation of client results" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **#5 Solve constraints quickly — become a diagnostician; results are money math.** Reverse-engineer the funnel from the revenue goal using benchmarks. Worked example: +$50k/mo at $2,500 AOV → 20 patients → ~67 consults (30% close) → ~556 leads (12% booking) → ~$6,667 ad spend ($12 CPL). The **constraint-fix loop:** (1) identify the KPI below goal, (2) determine which pillar holds it, (3) implement the specific fix, (4) measure and repeat — distinguishing a CONSTRAINT (below target) from an OPTIMIZATION (already at target). Wait ~$300–400 of spend before a definitive diagnosis.
  → Lesson: "5.Foundation of client results" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Quality IMPROVES at scale, it doesn't drop (Tesla vs the guy in a garage).** Refusing to sign clients to "protect fulfillment" is a disservice to existing clients — more clients = more ad spend = more data = faster discovery of what works. An agency running 100 local businesses at $150k/mo finds winners ~100x faster than a solo at $1,500/mo; clients hire you partly to leverage that collective testing power. Aim for 80–90% of work identical client-to-client.
  → Lesson: 7.SVD/Fundamentals — "6.Client fulfillment overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]

### The fulfillment-model spectrum (DIY / DWY / DFY)
- **All three sell the SAME outcome — they differ only in speed, ease, and probability of success. Start at the furthest end of DFY.** DIY = info only (lowest price/involvement/success); DWY = info + guidance + accountability; DFY = you do the work (highest price/involvement/success, biggest moat, best skill-building). Within DFY there's a spectrum: just generating leads vs also running a call center to nurture leads→bookings (the big value/moat jump, at the cost of operational complexity). **DWY/DIY success depends on the *market's* ability to execute** — the "DWY trap" is selling self-implementation to a non-tech-savvy market. Treat DWY/DIY as **downsells** (same marketing/sales, the closer just diagnoses the bucket); don't add one below ~$50–100k/mo or before you've gotten ≥10 clients the outcome.
  → Lesson: 7.SVD/Fundamentals — "2.The 3 main fulfillment models" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Origin story for the "market's ability to execute" rule:** a mastermind leader told Cameron (then ~$40k/mo) to drop DFY and just sell a course to med spas. He built a strong DWY — and it flopped because his non-tech-savvy market couldn't execute (he had to teach them to log into Facebook button-by-button). Add a call center when competitors offer it, all clients ask for it, and clients' own ability to call leads is low.
  → Lesson: "2.The 3 main fulfillment models" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Same fulfillment system ports across niches; don't over-engineer before you've sold.** The brick-and-mortar principle is identical (ad → call lead → book → free consult → close → upsell), so Cameron launches in new niches with no fulfillment infra yet — you figure it out once you've closed because you won't let a client down ("don't think 50 steps ahead before you've cracked step one"). Niches he runs on the SAME med-spa system: eye surgery, sleep apnea, ketamine, wound care, pain management, foundation repair, custom home builders, water filtration (high-ticket $3–5k, in-and-out).
  → Founder calls: "agency check in" ; "constraint diagnosis framework" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

### Constraint diagnosis (the lever sets per pillar)
- **Diagnose top-down, then drill to the contributing metric.** High-level metrics (cost-per-booking/offer/acquisition) aren't actionable; break them into inputs (CPL, lead-to-booking %, cost-per-intro, show rate, intro-to-close rate). Worked example from raw numbers: $1,500 spent, 55 leads, 10 appts, 7 shows, 3 closes → CPL $27, booking 18%, show 70%, close 42%, cash-upfront 50% → glaring bottleneck is CPL (furthest from goal) → leak is lead-gen → pull creative/offer/copy. Live dashboard tells: 3.85% schedule rate + $162 cost-per-booking → booking constraint; 13% pick-up rate (vs healthy ~39%) → spam number / wrong area code; ~55-min speed-to-lead (60 = timed out) → call-center efficiency.
  → Lesson: "Constraint diagnosis (CSM module)" · [COURSE-HIGH] · also "6.Client fulfillment overview"
- **The levers per constraint (keep ~4–5 fixes per pillar so a struggling client always has a next move):**
  - *CPL (lead-gen leak):* offer too expensive (>$400 → expect ~$15 CPL; >$17 → change offer); refresh 6+ creatives every 3–4 days across angles; expand targeting (~30km rural); ad spend ≥$50/day & check FB spend cap; move to a low-intent bare opt-in page; remove unnecessary qualifying questions.
  - *Booking rate:* technical (leads in pipeline, correct booking link, texts firing, redirect, calendar availability, correct AREA CODE); pick-up rate (<20% → spam-flagged number → new number/area code); speed-to-contact (target sub-10 min); ≥4 open days w/ multiple slots in next 10; deposit resistance → card-on-file w/ no-show fee (warning: lowers show); inspect disqualified pipeline; worst case change offer/angle.
  - *Show rate (<75% or cancels >10%):* card-on-file may be causing cancels; not enough / too-far-out availability (booking >7 days out lowers show); reminders with Google-Maps screenshot + local landmarks; out-of-control factors (bad reviews, no website, hard-to-find location) → help the client fix.
  - *Close rate (<40% or avg plan <$1,500):* is the client filling the stat sheet with DETAILED reasons (no notes = can't help); route common objections to objection-handling videos (objections are smokescreens ~99% of the time — the "open-wallet" shit-test: "how much in the bank / on credit?"); same-day bonus mini-treatment for a quick win; 3-tier package pricing $800–$5,000 + financing; tie-down/deposit in the first appointment (BamFam); send the proven sales-process video.
  → Lesson: "Constraint diagnosis (CSM module)" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Productize each fix AND its implementation.** SOP every foreseeable scenario (bad lead quality, out-of-service-area, etc.) listing every lever, so the media buyer just diagnoses and pulls a known lever; then productize the implementation so it's instant. "Money loves speed; volume negates risk" — you need endless reps to fix a campaign, so the biggest lever (creative) should be instant (AI video removed the 3–5 day external-editor wait).
  → Founder call: "Fulfillment, retention and lifetime value" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]
- **Never take "the leads are bad" at face value — clients are like toddlers.** Self-serving bias means clients credit themselves for wins and blame you for losses, so they'll always complain about lead quality; combined with the referral-vs-paid gap you can never fully solve it, only continually educate. Look at the data — usually they're bad at closing or not calling leads. The Yelp/Google trap: a client closed 2 jobs on $10/day Yelp but zero Facebook leads in 1–2 months — don't pivot your model off one client; on Google/Yelp the prospect researched more so CAC is 3–5x and they'll bitch anyway ("why so much vs $10/lead on Facebook?").
  → Lesson: "6.Client fulfillment overview" · [COURSE-HIGH] · reinforced [COURSE-MEDIUM] ("client retention and product pricing")

### Fulfillment economics & team structure
- **Price off COGS (labor) at a 5–7x minimum multiple — it's a floor, not a target.** Map the productized journey → assign a role + DRI to each step → estimate billable hours × hourly rate = COGS per client (use the onboarding/worst-case month) → multiply ≥5x (≈75% gross margin) to leave room for overhead, shared managers, churn, commissions. Example: ~18 hrs/client → ~$400 COGS → minimum ~$2,000/mo retainer.
  → Lesson: 7.SVD/Fundamentals — "7. Fulfillment economics at scale" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Capacity = available billable hours ÷ hours-per-offer; the lowest number is your bottleneck role.** Full-timer ~160 hrs/mo; use 80% billable (128 hrs) as the default hedge (head-down roles 85–90%). Hire the next person at ~80% of a role's capacity so acquisition never has to slow. The first constraint role is usually the video editor (highest hrs/client, front-loaded month 1). Manager capacity scales by *people managed*, not clients. The only four things a CSM should do: **Retain, Resell, Refer, Review.**
  → Lesson: "7. Fulfillment economics at scale" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Scale by cloning pods, not by growing one chaotic team.** A pod is "a business inside the business" running ~40–50 clients, capacity set by its highest-capacity role (usually CSM), incentivized on its own results/churn/LTV. Pod-specific roles (CSM/lead, media buyer, tech/onboarding VA, call agents) work one pod; shared roles (PM, call-center manager, fulfillment/ops manager, tech experts) span pods and are hired past ~$50–100k/mo. Scaling 40→60/80 clients is mostly "double what you already have": add a Team column on every board + pod filtering + per-pod KPIs (churn, grace-periods, upsells, referrals, reviews) on the CEO dashboard — don't reinvent the system. Below ~50 clients, skip pods; 0–10 clients hire a "black cat VA" (a clone of you) before specialists. Decentralize so problems route to a manager, and only the hardest penetrate up to the owner.
  → Lesson: "7. Fulfillment economics at scale" · [COURSE-HIGH] · reinforced [COURSE-MEDIUM] ("scaling operations and mm planning" ; "team management and client retention")
- **"Fake capacity" is usually inefficiency, not real capacity.** When someone says they're maxed, run a time audit (record 7–8 hrs / shadow on Zoom all day) before hiring. Cameron's CSM-shadow audit is "the highest-ROI-on-time move he ever did" — at ~20 clients he spent ~2 days screen-sharing and noting what they did wrong / what systems were missing. Proof it's about systems not a hard number: his first CSM panicked at 15 clients, second handled 30 then panicked, third managed 80. Common tells: CSMs building funnels/editing ads, media buyers doing client comms, no SOPs/templates, 30%+ time in meetings. Truly at capacity only when near billable-hour limit + role-pure work + full SOP/AI usage + no obvious inefficiency.
  → Lesson: "7. Fulfillment economics at scale" · [COURSE-HIGH] · reinforced [COURSE-MEDIUM] ("scaling operations and mm planning")
- **Meeting-cost discipline + build-vs-buy talent.** Every meeting costs money (60 min × 10 people @ $10/hr ≈ $100); cut 60→30→5–10 where you can. Build (hire less-experienced + train; cheaper/slower) vs buy (experienced, pricier short-term) is a cash-flow/risk call, not a revenue threshold — "skill is easy to train; the will is the hard part."
  → Lesson: "7. Fulfillment economics at scale" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Hiring/comp mechanics (founder calls):** incentivize a media buyer on the controllable metric (cost-per-result, computed off the fulfillment tracker for their pod). Cheap media buyers can work (his first was $400/mo → $800 after a year → now a killer); perfect ready-made buyers in your exact workflow rarely exist — pay-cheap-and-train or pay-up-and-still-maybe-get-shit. **Never hire a media buyer who has his own clients / sees you as a client** (short-term only; record yourself doing the ads to build the in-house SOP) — prefer a full-time VA + good SOPs at $800–1,000/mo over a "killer" freelancer juggling contracts. Keep diagnosis ABOVE the media buyer (the fulfillment manager diagnoses; media buyers refresh/implement). One SOP-follower should execute multi-step changes (e.g. price change = ad + GHL variables + script) — don't split across media-buyer + CSM. **Don't let CSMs chase billing** — use a separate billing VA as the "bad cop" so the relationship stays non-transactional.
  → Founder calls: Marbella Q4'24 Session 1 ; "team management and client retention" ; "tools, hiring and team" ; "team hiring and expectations" ; "product pricing and team management" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

### Onboarding as an assembly line (backend is where churn is made)
- **Automate onboarding to compress time-to-value — technically launch-ready in ~48h, but Cameron deliberately sets 7 days for headroom while signing volume.** The SOP wires GHL + Make + Monday + Slack so the new-client form auto-triggers Slack channels, CRM/sub-account creation, Drive folders, per-department tasks, A2P kickoff, contract dispatch, and QA. The full technical build is ~2–3 hrs and gets you ~80–90% of the way; the rest is personalization. The take-what-you-need ladder: the full automated build is "completely a distraction" sub ~$20–40k/mo or with one CSM — build the manual checklists first, automate when you're doing >~10 units/mo and it gets sloppy.
  → Lesson: 7.SVD/Automated onboarding — "1. Process overview" · [COURSE-HIGH] · "Live onboarding buildout"
- **"Death by a thousand paper cuts" — ~90% of onboarding failures originate on the BACKEND, not the calls.** One wrong custom value (the address) cascades: leads booked to the wrong location → bad review → angry client → churn. So the backend must be SOP'd "so an idiot could go through it" AND have a QA layer (the last line of defense). The backend splits into four domains, each its own board/SOP: Quick steps (PM admin), Tech (GHL/funnel by tech VA), Ads (media buyer), Launch (final QA + go-live).
  → Lesson: "Backend onboarding" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **The kickoff form (not the onboarding form) is the build trigger — the single biggest accuracy lever.** Three forms, three jobs: (1) New client form = filled by the closer the moment they pay (triggers all downstream; no form = no commission); (2) Onboarding form = filled by the client pre-call (expect it half-wrong — clients are "lazy and half-arse it"); (3) Kickoff form = filled by CSM + client live, the *vetted* version, the real backend trigger. Waiting for the validated kickoff form before building cut onboarding errors ~50%. Trigger-link attribution prefills the boring fields so the CSM never re-types. Use a numeric **Client ID as the universal join key** across Monday/GHL/Slack (name-matching breaks on a single typo).
  → Lesson: "1. Process overview" ; "Backend onboarding" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **The onboarding call exists to create the AHA moment** ("my goal is just math"). On-call calculator: fill BLUE (your benchmarks: CPL, lead-to-booking, cancellation, show, qualified-appt %) and RED (client-controlled: close %, price, cash upfront, offer, ad spend) → it outputs exactly what must be true to hit the goal, surfacing realistic projections so you never set unrealistic goals. Also: establish leadership, gather assets, set up the comms channel, secure commitment to the sales training, run the database-reactivation quick win, and BAMFAM the launch call (~5–7 days out). Keep it ~1 hr.
  → Lesson: "4.Client journey overview" ; "1. Process overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]
- **Don't auto-book the onboarding call at signup; have the CSM CALL immediately and push them through each step.** After testing, Cameron stopped auto-booking it (clients vanished before an unused booked hour). Pair with a post-sale 2-slide presentation ("here's what you need to do") + a hard deadline, level-set as launch risk: "if you don't get this done by 8pm today it delays your launch — do you understand?" ## Decision rules
- **If a client is churning,** first diagnose *which* pillar broke — Results or Relationship — and fix that one; the strong opposite pillar is buying you time, don't waste it.
- **If churn is the binding constraint,** fix it before scaling acquisition — it opens margin and raises the client cap; signing harder without fixing churn just keeps you capped.
- **If results are off,** diagnose top-down then drill: find the single bucket below benchmark across Leads→Bookings→Shows→Closes, patch that one leak, re-measure — don't fix everything at once. Always give the client a lever, never "it's all on you."
- **If tempted to diagnose before ~$300–400 of ad spend,** wait — no signal yet.
- **If a prospect shows budget scarcity (~$1k to their name) or is a pain before paying,** expect bottom-20% and filter them out — don't take them for cash flow.
- **If a client is trending toward the bottom 20%,** run a ~30-day improvement plan with clear criteria; if no change and mindset is the issue, make a clean break (even a partial refund). Be proactive — the moment they trend below KPI, tell them and change something.
- **If a client is top 20%,** white-glove + upsell + harvest the testimonial/case study, then analyze their traits and clone them (retarget messaging + feed to the pixel). Never tell a client which bucket they're in.
- **If you can deliver in 7 days, promise 2–4 weeks** — build the buffer, kill the negative expectation gap.
- **If the client fixates on front-end cash,** reframe to CAC:LTGP over 6–12 months and restructure to pull cash forward (prepaid / financing) so they're cash-profitable inside 30 days.
- **If choosing a fulfillment model,** start at the furthest end of DFY; only sell DWY/DIY once the market can execute and you've gotten ≥10 people the result — and as downsells, not below ~$50–100k/mo.
- **If a client asks for an extra service (SEO, social) while ads work,** say no / pause it — don't add solutions to problems you don't have until past $100k/mo (then prefer white-label).
- **If pricing a retainer,** set it at 5–7x per-client labor COGS minimum — it's a floor; below it you can't survive churn or reinvest.
- **If deciding the next hire,** hire the role whose (billable hrs ÷ hrs-per-offer) capacity is lowest — the true bottleneck, not the loudest complainer.
- **If a team member claims they're at capacity,** run a time audit / shadow them first; assume inefficiency until billable-limit + role-pure work + full SOP usage are all true.
- **If approaching ~40–50 clients,** stop scaling the flat team and clone a pod ("double what you have"); add a department manager once 2–3 people report into one function. Below ~50, skip pods entirely.
- **If hiring a media buyer,** never hire one who has his own clients / sees you as a client; prefer a full-time VA + good SOPs ($800–1k/mo) and keep diagnosis above the buyer. · [COURSE-MEDIUM]
- **If a churn-set client has a legitimate reason (your mistake / a bad month),** offer a 2–4 week grace period — a free month at ~$750 COGS beats ~$2k to acquire a replacement; deny grace if they're just finessing a free month.
- **If a competing client exists in the same area,** take them anyway and run a DIFFERENT service to avoid direct conflict — don't DQ same-area leads. · [COURSE-MEDIUM]
- **If ending any client call,** BAMFAM — book the next before they leave (two specific time options); never open-ended.
- **If a client hits a significant win,** activate Advocacy immediately for referral/testimonial/case study — don't wait for day 60–90.
- **If building the backend,** trigger off the vetted KICKOFF form, not the client's onboarding form; build manual checklists first and only automate past ~$20–40k/mo or >~10 units/mo.
- **If the ad account has no active payment method,** STOP the build and notify the CSM (delay incoming).
- **If A2P isn't approved yet but you want to launch,** start CALLING leads (the phone works); only SMS is blocked. · [COURSE-MEDIUM]
- **If optimizing the ad set,** use conversion event Lead unless you reliably get 3–4 daily schedule conversions; keep the ad with the lower cost-per-acquisition even if its CPL is higher.
- **If a live issue surfaces post-launch,** go back to the completed QA checklist, ask why it slipped, fix the SOP.
