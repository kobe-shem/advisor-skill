# Cameron England — Lead-gen pillar (distilled)

## Use this card for
How to acquire B2B agency-style clients predictably: paid-ad strategy and media buying, offer
validation on cold traffic, funnel architecture (lead form vs landing page vs VSL), the
nurture machine that turns opt-ins into shows, show-rate engineering, lead-quality diagnosis,
and the "find the one constraint and fix it" CEO discipline. Cameron's frame is local/B2B
lead-gen agencies with a small TAM — his media-buying advice is explicitly calibrated to that,
not to high-TAM ecom.

Does NOT cover: what happens once the prospect is on the call (the close, objection handling,
pricing the deal on the call) → that's the **sales** pillar. Setter/closer human-process
training and the call center build → **call-center** pillar. Tracker/attribution build
mechanics live here at a doctrine level but the deep tooling SOPs (Hyros, Windsor, full-stack
tracker) are tracking lessons. Never use this card for the user's own pricing/packaging/ICP/
guarantee — those are mutable facts of their business, kept separate from doctrine.

## Core doctrine (distilled, evergreen)

- **You are the first setter and the first closer; you earn the right to delegate by mastering
  the role.** Take the first 20-30 (he also says 30-50 when launching a new offer) sales calls
  yourself — no one finds the blind spots in your funnel faster than you, and a hired closer
  lacks your context. The two most profitable activities at every revenue level are *generating*
  calls and *closing* them. Marketing leverage ladder: outbound (lowest, do it broke) → ads →
  delegate setting/closing → marketing manager; hold onto generating-calls longest because
  nobody cares about your lead flow as much as you. He did all setting + selling himself to
  ~$80k/mo, kept selling to ~$100k/mo with just a setter qualifying, and still steps back onto
  calls to find messaging/sales constraints.
  → Lesson: A - Fundamentals — "Acquisition process breakdown" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: B - Ads — "Ad budget and offer validation" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Crack ONE acquisition channel to ~$100k/mo before diversifying.** ~80-90% paid ads (most
  predictable + scalable), ~10-20% referrals (a byproduct of a good product). One channel, one
  funnel, one offer to seven figures. Don't add cold email/SMS/calling out of the gate — that's
  a fear/avoidance move; the brain over-complicates. Only diversify channels later for an exit
  (acquirers want it). Outbound-to-scale is "long, big, hairy"; test ONE thing, never hire 5
  agencies at once.
  → Lesson: A - Fundamentals — "Acquisition process breakdown" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Founder call — "Optimizing operations" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

- **The 4-step acquisition machine + its KPIs.** Every client passes four gates: Generate Leads
  (cost-per-qualified-lead) → Get leads to book (lead-to-booking %) → Get bookings to show
  (show-up %) → Get shows to close (close rate / upfront-cash %). Visualize it as a pipe; the
  founder's whole job is "whack-a-mole" — find the single biggest leak, patch it, find the next.
  A constraint always exists; solving one surfaces the next (fix lead flow → exposes a DQ
  problem). Cameron also reduces it to "the only four constraints in paid acquisition: leads,
  bookings, shows, closes."
  → Lesson: E - Lead nurture & constraints — "Diagnosing and fixing constraints" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: A - Fundamentals — "Paid ad KPI's and Math" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Three-layer KPI hierarchy + the magnifying glass.** Layer 1 (lagging, pays the bills, hard
  to read short-term): front-end cash ROAS, contracted ROAS, CPA. Layer 2 (week-to-week): CPL,
  cost-per-booking, show rate, cost-per-qualified-show, close rate, upfront-cash %. Layer 3
  (leading/daily early-warning): lead-to-booking %, DQ %, CTR, CPM, VSL play rate. **Golden
  rule: if Layer 1 is profitable, do NOT touch a winning campaign because a Layer 2/3 vanity
  metric looks ugly** ("I do not care if a layer-2 metric is below KPI if my layer-1 is crushing
  it"). Diagnose by zooming from a broken Layer-1 metric down to L2 then L3 to find the exact
  constraint and WHY; you can also work upstream (a bad L3 metric flags a future L1 problem).
  → Lesson: A - Fundamentals — "Paid ad KPI's and Math" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Macro vs micro constraints (the cardboard-vs-steel-pipe model).** Micro = one isolated leak
  in an otherwise profitable steel pipe (CPL, lead-to-booking, show rate, CTR). Macro = the whole
  cardboard pipe leaks (Layer-1 ROAS/CPA broken, every downstream metric off) — patching
  individual leaks with more cardboard is wasted; you must change the material (offer/messaging).
  Macro is almost always a broken offer or misaligned messaging attracting the wrong people. Many
  micro leaks compounding IS a macro constraint — but if the leads themselves are wrong, fix the
  root and the micro problems resolve downstream. **Only fix a micro constraint once macro is
  healthy** — patching small leaks on a 1.2x ROAS is "rearranging deck chairs on the Titanic."
  When multiple constraints exist, attack the highest-impact, easiest-to-implement one first
  (lifting close rate 10→30% triples client flow; lifting lead-to-booking 50→70% is a small lift
  — do the close rate first).
  → Lesson: E - Lead nurture & constraints — "Diagnosing and fixing constraints" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Weekly pulse, monthly deep dive — and move FAST.** The single biggest mistake is not making
  changes fast enough. Operating ritual: set monthly targets (reverse-engineered from "I want to
  sign X clients" → required spend → leads/bookings/shows/closes by benchmark rates); each
  Monday fill the actual last-7-days metrics into the budget sheet, find the reddest gap (= the
  constraint), assign a specific action item to a specific person with a due date. Lean on
  *leading* indicators weekly (can't read Layer-1 off 7 noisy days — "regression to the mean");
  monthly deep dive (2-4 hrs) is where you find MACRO constraints and look at lagging/L1 trends.
  "Learning is a change in behavior" (citing Hormozi) — every audit must end with action items
  (today / week / month / quarter), or it's insanity. Operator math to get right: close rate =
  closed ÷ SHOWED (not ÷ offers made); CPA must include the 10-15% sales commission; net
  operating profit = cash − labor − marketing − overhead (exclude "other").
  → Lesson: E - Lead nurture & constraints — "Diagnosing and fixing constraints" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Set a "burn budget" before you launch — it's an investment in data, not a cost.** Decide the
  max you're 100% OK lighting on fire before spending a dollar; there's an unavoidable gap (1-4
  weeks) where you bleed before profit. This is the "Valley of Despair" (borrowed from Hormozi):
  new advertisers start optimistic, hit the valley ("ads don't work"), and most quit there,
  staying stuck below $10-30k/mo. Sit in the pain and diagnose the constraint instead of shutting
  ads off. (Cameron spent ~$10k before the first close on his own program offer because of show-
  rate, fit, and closer issues that surfaced — atypical, but illustrates the gap.)
  → Lesson: B - Ads — "Ad budget and offer validation" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Two validation paths, same loop.** Path 1 (fast/well-funded, >$50k bank, value time>money):
  ~$500/day B2B ($250 B2C), launch 3-5 campaigns split evenly by *creative angle*, 4-6 days, cut
  anything outside KPI; broad/interest targeting only (no complex/lookalike at first — ~90% of
  his best performers). Path 2 (bootstrapped, <$50k bank): $50-100/day across 1-2 campaigns
  (everything in ONE campaign if <$100/day), burn budget 1.5-3k, supplemented with sweat equity
  (call every lead in 5 min, personal Looms, no-brainer/bait-and-switch offer, lead form not
  landing page, organic alongside). Either way: test → gather data → compare to pre-set KPI →
  form a hypothesis → iterate in 4-6 day cycles (~3 iterations in ~3 weeks usually cracks it).
  Validated only when you repeatedly generate leads/sales at a ≥1:3 CAC-to-lifetime-gross-profit
  ratio. If you can't spend $50/day for 60 days without pain, you're not ready — rip outbound
  first (or run both and reinvest outbound cash into ads). Don't hire a setter/closer/marketing-
  manager on an unvalidated offer — you'd set them up for failure.
  → Lesson: B - Ads — "Ad budget and offer validation" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **"Facebook ads don't work" is almost never true — it's offer, then creative/copy, then media
  buying.** Three (and only three) reasons ads fail given adequate market size, in that order of
  likelihood: offer problem, creative/copy problem, media-buying problem. Media buying is *least*
  likely because Meta's broad targeting + AI is now very good. Fix the offer first (an ads-ready
  offer is crystal-clear, solves a burning problem, has proven demand), then creative/copy (where
  most problems live), then targeting (broad/basic-interest scales to $40k/mo+; overcomplicated
  audiences are a self-inflicted wound). The 80/20 of paid ads = creative + offer — "Chad-
  vertiser" (broad, let Facebook target, focus on creative+offer) beats the "virgin complicated
  media buyer" obsessing over CBO/ABO/DCT/lookalikes. Cameron didn't know CBO/ABO/DCT difference
  until ~6 months before the Andromeda call. Validating product-market fit on cold traffic is the
  hard part; scaling it is easy.
  → Lesson: A - Fundamentals — "Ad pitfalls and key tips" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: B - Ads — "Creating your ads" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Cold leads buy the OFFER; organic leads buy YOU.** Organic leads know/like/trust you and buy
  regardless of offer/guarantee — which is why people get blindsided going paid and discover
  their offer doesn't solve an adamant problem. On cold traffic the offer, copy, creative, and
  proof do all the work. Organic isn't scalable/predictable; paid lets you reverse-engineer
  client count. Organic content is fuel-on-the-fire, not a primary channel — only double down on
  it when the market voraciously consumes content (biz-op/unaware audiences). The market-
  sophistication pyramid: only ~3% of any market is in the buying window; ~7-9 hours of content
  consumption builds enough trust to buy.
  → Lesson: A - Fundamentals — "Ad pitfalls and key tips" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Make decisions on data, never emotion — the 3-5x rule.** Spend 3-5x your target KPI across
  4-6 days before judging an ad. On small budgets the rule holds, the timeline stretches (10-14
  days) and you lean on leading indicators (CTR, CPL). Small-budget early-kill exception: ~$200
  spent, CTR sub-1%, zero leads → kill early rather than burn the full $500. Don't impulsively
  kill on 24h data (regression to the mean → knee-jerk decisions); a low-cost-per-call ad with no
  closes yet may just be lagging (esp. a 2-call process). Optimize for the *highest* layer metric:
  high CPL but good cost-per-booked-call → leave it; high cost-per-booked-call but great ROAS →
  leave it. "Most people fix shit until they're broke." The end goal is profitability, not a
  perfect dashboard.
  → Lesson: A - Fundamentals — "Ad pitfalls and key tips" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Ads = Offer + Copy + Creative; copy/video = Hook / Body / CTA, and ~80-90% of effort goes to
  the hook.** Average Meta attention is ~1.8s (people scroll ~356 ft/day), so for video make
  ~5 hooks per 1 body (editor cuts each hook onto the same body, near-invisible seam — require
  hook/body congruency), test all 5, find the winner by lowest cost-per-result, make ~5 sub-
  variations of the winner, repeat. Write the BODY so ANY hook ties on cleanly (open with the
  mechanism, e.g. "me and my US-based patient sales team will book your calendar with qualified
  clients…"). Video part lengths: hook 3-5s (metric: hook rate / thru-play), body 20-80s (metric:
  hold rate / avg watch time), CTA 5-10s (metric: CTR). Direct ads (clear "book a call" CTA, ~99%
  of ads, 90% of attention) target Highly-/Solution-Aware (easiest, smallest slice); indirect/
  educational ads target Problem-Aware/Unaware. Test only ONE variable at a time. **Unique
  mechanism** = "the simultaneous reason everything they've tried failed AND why your solution is
  different," a specific named method. **Unspoken mechanism** = a practice competitors already do
  but nobody names on ads — being first to name it is what lets you scale.
  → Lesson: B - Ads — "Creating your ads" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: A - Fundamentals — "Acquisition process breakdown" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Start with image ads, scale into video (70/30).** Images get live in ~10 min, lower CPL,
  pair well with a no-brainer/pay-on-results offer, validate angles fast (~100 via Ideogram in
  ~10 min) — but fatigue faster and skew lower-quality. Video is king at scale (~70% budget),
  builds trust, brings cheaper CAC + better leads ~9/10 times, slower fatigue, harder for
  competitors to copy — but a ~4-7 day script→record→edit turnaround (a key reason to validate
  with images first). Keep images and video in SEPARATE budgets (separate CBOs or ABO ad sets) —
  don't let Facebook spend across both. RSA (Repeat Successful Actions) — when something wins,
  screenshot it and immediately make ~4 variations. Note the honest counter-result: meme-hook ads
  underperformed and he stopped running them.
  → Lesson: B - Ads — "Creating your ads" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: C - Ad Scaling — "Scale phase 1 ($0-$100/day)" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **B2B local lead-gen has small TAM, so scale the ACCOUNT, not the ad (horizontal/variation
  scaling).** Small TAM makes the account "frail" — it amplifies luck, "initial conditions"
  (Facebook seeds each ad to a small group whose reaction dictates the rest, driving CPM/CPC; he
  duplicated one ad 4x identically and Facebook spent most on the *worst* one at $240/lead while
  another got 9 leads at ~$90), and margins of error. So the YouTube/ecom "spend $10k/day on one
  winning ad" (vertical scaling) breaks small-TAM B2B — it nudges the algorithm and burns
  creatives too fast. Instead spread budget across multiple campaigns/ad sets running for weeks,
  and scale by **near variation** (same message, swap color/font/background) and **far variation**
  (exact same words, completely different visual layout — his favorite, because it re-rolls
  "initial conditions" on proven messaging without burning the angle). Treat every setting (CBO
  vs ABO, lead-form vs landing page, a survey question) as a *tool that fixes a specific problem*,
  not "better." There are NO absolutes — it's Vegas/probabilistic; make the highest-probability
  next move and pull the trigger fast.
  → Lesson: C - Ad Scaling — "Media buying fundamentals" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Three-phase scale model.** Phase 1 ($0→$100/day): find winners (can be over in 3-4 days).
  Phase 2 ($100→$300/day): turn winners into near/far variations + the audience-stacking hack.
  Phase 3 ($300→$1,000+/day): max variation + max creative volume. **Million-dollar thought
  experiment (Phase 3):** imagine all your direct + indirect competitors merged into one entity
  profitably spending tens of thousands/day to your exact ICP (proof is in the ad library) — study
  that merged entity and the answer is relentless **variation** (styles/tones/faces/colors/copies/
  offers across the awareness ladder) plus huge **volume** of ads. New/brand-new ideas matter more
  as spend increases because concepts fatigue faster at higher budgets.
  → Lesson: C - Ad Scaling — "Scale phase 1 ($0-$100/day)" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: C - Ad Scaling — "Scale phase 3 ($300+/day)" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **When something works, DO NOT TOUCH IT.** The single biggest scale-killer is tinkering with
  budget/targeting/copy on a working ad — it re-rolls the dice and usually tanks it. To scale a
  proven campaign, *launch a new one* (don't reset the learning phase). Cautionary tale: a founder
  at ~$300 cost-per-call swapped in a VSL + Typeform/Calendly and it jumped to ~$1,000. Squeeze
  the winner before chasing novelty: "test more ads" does NOT mean test something completely
  different — replicate your best ad with new faces/hooks/settings. A winning *primary text* can
  run for months/years (refresh the first couple of lines to reset performance); headlines die
  faster and rotate more.
  → Lesson: C - Ad Scaling — "Media buying fundamentals" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Founder call — "Sales, team and ads bottlenecks" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

- **Phase-1 launch structure + "fix the nearest bottleneck."** Start: 1 CBO campaign $100/day+,
  2 ad sets (broad vs targeting+, same 3 ads, only audience differs — let CBO pick the winning
  audience), 3-5 ads each = 1 creative × 2-3 copies × 2-3 headlines, lead form with minimal DQ,
  ad-set min $50/day. CBO when you need Facebook to find the audience; ABO once you have a winning
  audience and want to split-test creatives. **Manual placements only** (Feeds, Stories, Reels —
  everything else off; junk placements deliver cheap garbage), kill all Advantage+ AI
  enhancements, min age ~25, set country. The flow ladder is Clicks → Leads → Bookings → Shows;
  always test the fix to your *immediate* constraint, not a downstream one (don't fix show rate
  before you have consistent leads). A winner = ~5+ conversions at a cost you're happy with, even
  if baseline stats look ugly. Build a custom Ads-Manager column preset (cost-per-schedule, CPL,
  CTR(link), CPC, CPM, ThruPlays, **hook rate** = 3s-plays÷impressions, **hold rate** =
  ThruPlays÷3s-plays) and optimize on the last 5-7 days, never on yesterday alone.
  → Lesson: C - Ad Scaling — "Scale phase 1 ($0-$100/day)" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: B - Ads — "Facebook ads setup" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Andromeda-era structure (current best, live coaching): broad CBO, one ad set, 30-50 diverse
  ads.** Above $100-200/day → CBO, ONE ad set, 30-50 *concept-diverse* ads, fully broad, 5 ad
  copies + 5 headlines; sub-$100/day → 8-12 diverse creatives (up from the old 4). Distinguish a
  **concept** (a completely new angle/selling point) from a **variation** (a small hook/thumbnail/
  color tweak — Meta buckets these together; only new concepts give unique signals). Exploration =
  launch 20-40 diverse concepts to find winners; exploitation = make variations around a winner.
  On launch Meta dumps spend on ~3-4 of the 30-50; if they perform leave them (RSA), if they die
  you still have ~20+ untouched to test, if a top ad spent 2-3x KPI with no result turn it off so
  spend flows elsewhere. "Netflix for old men": swapping only hooks = stocking only war films;
  new concepts = stocking war films + Love Island + romance → you capture the whole market.
  → Lesson: B - Ads — "Andromeda masterclass live" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

- **Funnels: lead form vs landing page is a flow-vs-control tradeoff; default to the MVF first.**
  Minimum Viable Funnel = opt-in → schedule → thank-you, launchable in 1-2 hrs, lowest friction
  for fast data. Lead forms = higher flow / lower CPL / lower intent / worse quality (Facebook
  targets "Learn more" clickers + accidental clicks, can't scale past ~£100-300/day before quality
  collapses); landing pages = higher intent / more control (VSL, proof, multi-format) / higher CPL
  but often lower cost-per-qualified-lead. **Cost-per-qualified-lead, not CPL, is the real target**
  — "I'd rather have a higher cost-per-lead and a lower cost-per-acquisition." But net cost-per-
  booking is often *lower* on the high-volume lead form because flow makes up for a weaker lead-
  to-booking rate — judge on cost-per-booking, not CPL in isolation. Four funnel types map to
  awareness/sophistication (Type 1 direct opt-in MVF → Type 2 VSL → Type 3 free-training → Type 4
  lead-magnet/self-liquidating). His Direct-to-Application (no VSL) is his best lead-gen performer;
  for a simple/self-explanatory offer, put the VSL as a PRE-CALL on the thank-you page rather than
  gating the opt-in. The ONE exception to lead-form-first: highly sophisticated markets (SaaS,
  agencies, ecom) need a landing page + VSL from day one. "Repeat successful actions" — people who
  moved to a fancier funnel often flopped vs their lead form; validate, don't assume sophistication
  = better. Opt-in psychology: a micro-commitment opt-in BEFORE the schedule page lifts follow-
  through; use a question-at-a-time *survey* over a long form; "confused mind never buys" — one
  action per page, no nav links, congruent guarantee across ad/VSL/offer.
  → Lesson: D - Funnels — "Funnel types breakdown" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: A - Fundamentals — "B2B Snapshot" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The VSL = a sales call on autopilot; build only at a quality constraint, after ~10-20 calls.**
  Two frameworks: Direct-Offer VSL (Hook → big claim + guarantee → 3 key results/milestones →
  proof → CTA) and Objection-Prevention VSL (built from 20-40 real sales-call transcripts,
  dismantling objections in descending frequency order). Use the "3 key results" structure where
  each result answers one objection then immediately answers the next second-order objection it
  creates (objection chaining). Length by audience: affluent/sophisticated/skeptical doctors →
  short 2-10 min (he runs ~3-4); low-sophistication biz-op needing storytelling → long 15-45 min.
  Use language they respect (capital, asset, ROI, systems) for sophisticated, cash/income language
  for B2C. Implement a VSL only on two signals: lots of unqualified leads wasting call time, or
  "clueless show-ups" turning sales calls into education calls — otherwise launch without one (a
  direct opt-in funnel with no VSL can stay the best performer; only add a VSL at a CPA ceiling).
  Read Wistia analytics: a sharp early drop-off (filtering non-ICP)
  then a FLAT line is good; random mid-video drop = boring/not resonating.
  → Lesson: D - Funnels — "Mastering VSL's" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The Proof Hierarchy + the Halo effect.** Belief is a doubt↔certainty spectrum (the "modern
  trust recession"); move them toward certainty with the highest-trust proof you have. Proof
  hierarchy (hardest to fake = most certainty): in-person video / live event / live revenue-
  refresh on screen (top) → Zoom video testimonials → text screenshots / written reviews (bottom).
  Order all social proof best-to-worst. The Halo effect: first impression of your ad/funnel/player
  correlates with perceived service quality — a marketing agency's own marketing must look
  premium (drives the Wistia embed + brand-congruent mandate). Multi-format every asset for the
  three learning styles (video + burned-in subtitles + a pulled text quote). Two best VIDEO case
  studies under the opt-in beat ~100 testimonials. With no high-tier proof: lean on a unique
  mechanism backed by stats + your personal conviction/story, and a strong risk reversal — the
  less proof, the better the offer/guarantee must be.
  → Lesson: D - Funnels — "Mastering VSL's" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: D - Funnels — "Funnel setup and optimization" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The Thank-You page is the highest-leverage page in the funnel (a pre-selling machine, not a
  receipt).** Four pillars: (1) a 2-4 min pre-call video (hook with WIIFT in the first 30s →
  quick add-to-calendar walkthrough → address THE single biggest no-show driver as a story → point
  to objection-prevention videos → urgency with believable reasons), (2) an impossible-to-miss
  calendar confirmation, (3) objection-prevention videos ordered by frequency (face + the QUESTION
  as text below the play button, 4-8 of them, contextually sequenced), (4) credibility hammer
  (video case studies one-per-row, then image proof two-per-row, by proof hierarchy). Calendar
  hacks: sticky contact (pre-fill + hide fields so the only action is the commitment checkbox),
  dynamic availability capped at ~72hrs / 3 days, a "no reschedules offered" commitment checkbox.
  Implementing this took Pinpoint's show rate ~40% → ~65-70%; can be the difference between a 50%
  and 70% show rate + a ~5-10% close lift.
  → Lesson: D - Funnels — "Thank you page optimization" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Speed-to-lead is non-negotiable; lead decay is real.** Call EVERY lead within 5 minutes
  (cited: a lead is ~21x more likely to qualify, and ~100x more likely to respond/want-to-buy, at
  minute 5 vs minute 30) — even if they self-booked, to triage and push the call forward (often to
  same-day). The instant a lead opts in: a GHL opportunity is created, lead pushed to Facebook via
  Conversion API, a notification fires in the "new leads" Slack channel; the setter reacts with a
  checkmark and dials within 5 min. Track speed-to-lead and dials-per-lead on EVERY lead (pipe
  every event into a public Slack channel so you can audit on a micro level — don't trust "my
  leads ARE called in 5 min" without the data; that's the dieter who won't step on the scale).
  Cameron estimates <5% of his program actually calls within ~10 min. Selfie-video fallback when
  a lead doesn't pick up (proves a real person, mini pre-pitch). "Close the gap" — minimize the
  time between opt-in and a real sales conversation; collapse intro→demo into the same day.
  → Lesson: E - Lead nurture & constraints — "Lead nurture best practices" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The Value Hammer (WIIFT) + the interest spectrum — lead with value, never a selfish "book
  here."** Cold leads don't know/like/trust you and don't care about your opinion; frame every
  touchpoint around their outcome. Three lead states: Curious → Interested → Convicted (deals
  close at conviction; ~80% arrive curious/interested). The whole nurture machine has one job:
  brute-force-ascend them toward conviction fast. "Not convicted" ≠ "unqualified" — don't write
  off non-responders after 2-3 calls. Open with a resource tied to the constraint they named on
  the opt-in survey ("you put SALES as your biggest thing — I've got two things that might
  help…"), then book; same on setter confirmation calls (founder-name-drop → offer a resource →
  tease a "full market audit" → confirm the time → leave on a cliffhanger). This is the difference
  Cameron attributes to a ~40% vs ~70% show rate. The six levers of lead nurture: speed-to-lead,
  value hammer, sales-asset library, humanized messaging, multi-channel nurture, long-term value.
  → Lesson: E - Lead nurture & constraints — "Lead nurture best practices" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Humanize and multi-channel the nurture; arm the team with a sales-asset library.** Make
  automated messages feel manual: break into mini-texts with 3-10s wait steps; a ChatGPT GHL
  action that converts "January 14th 2pm 2025" → "Tuesday at 2pm" (dramatically raised call-
  confirmation rate); a ChatGPT action that takes the lead's city → "name the biggest competitor"
  → "wanted to chat about [Competitor]…" (response rates "through the roof"); a "Sent from my
  iPhone" follow-up email ~12 min after the openly-automated confirmation. Multi-channel: blue
  iMessage via SendBlue (lifted intro+demo show rate ~5%, but cycle numbers above ~200-300/day),
  ElevenLabs-cloned voice notes, personalized video for high-value leads (<5/day = no excuse),
  WhatsApp for Europe/UK only (always with an SMS fail-safe), setters follow leads on IG, a three-
  way iMessage group handoff (setter → closer) that lifts show rate. Build a centralized, ever-
  growing library (start from 3-5: a long-form client interview, a "why us" VSL, an FAQ video,
  objection-prevention videos, a process one-pager); mine it from sales-call transcripts (Fathom →
  Google Doc → .txt → ChatGPT "most common questions/objections, highest→lowest"); templatize in
  GHL (make a template once something recurs 3+ times); add an end-of-day report question to keep
  it compounding. **Pre-call nurture CAN be automated; POST-call follow-up must be MANUAL** (it
  must be contextual — Cameron tried automating it, it fails; closer owns a "hot list" Google
  Sheet). ~40% of closed deals come from follow-up. Mere-exposure: the more they see your face,
  the more they trust you.

- **The "Hammer Them" strategy — turn closers into cashiers by pre-selling in the booking
  window.** In the ~72hr window between booking and the call (peak interest), blitz the lead with
  ~10-20 pieces of content + up to ~6 emails/day, all valuable, organized into 3 buckets:
  Questions, Objections, Expectations (mined from sales-call transcripts via AI). Works via 4
  mechanisms: familiarity bias, mere-exposure effect, selling the NEXT step (showing up, not the
  end result), and pre-handling objections. Short-form framework (30-90s, one idea, NO CTA since
  already booked): hook → empathize with the pain → reframe/break the belief → solution → close.
  Long-form = authority via value density + storytelling + indirect proof of systems ("enough to
  know they need to buy, not enough to DIY"). Retarget natively in Meta (Engagement objective, CBO,
  max ThruPlays — the ONLY metric that matters is FREQUENCY): audience = booked-in-last-3-7-days
  (built from the pixel's "schedule" event, expanded to ≥100 people), exclude clients, one ad per
  ad set using an existing organic post with NO button/URL (feels like organic content), per-video
  exclusion of people who already watched ≥3s/≥10s. Target ~15-20x frequency on $10-25/day; judge
  on show rate, close rate, and shortened sales-cycle, not clicks. Trigger: build it when scale
  starts dragging show/quality down — if show rate already 80%+ and close 30%+ and your only gap is
  lead flow, it's a distraction (but build it now while you don't need it). Credited to Jeremy
  Haynes, adapted for agencies.
  → Lesson: E - Lead nurture & constraints — "Hammer them strategy" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: E - Lead nurture & constraints — "Hammer them technical setup" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Show rate is a stack of ~15 levers — pull the 3 immediate ones first.** Immediate: (1) shorten
  the booking window to max 72hrs (ideally same-day/2-day; the further out, the higher the no-
  show — flat tire, sick kid, talks to other agencies, decaying interest), (2) call-confirm every
  booking the minute it's booked AND the morning of, (3) dial in the thank-you page. Then layer:
  6-8+ daily slots (alert when <10 slots in next 72hrs), value-led confirmation calls with a
  *double tie-down* ("besides getting hit by a bus, any reason you couldn't make this?"), reframe
  the call as a named outcome session (not "demo/quick chat" — "patient generation call"), a
  "sexy" meeting agenda, a commitment checkbox, the same-day-reschedule rule (~90% who reschedule
  to another day no-show again), a strategic reciprocity gift (the "cousin Stacy cooked you
  dinner" analogy), low-tech meeting (Meet/phone over Zoom for non-techy industries), offering two
  specific times not "what works for you," and a branded Zoom waiting-room proof reel. Segment
  "call requested" vs "call confirmed" — manual confirmers show ~90%. Advanced (15-20+ calls/wk):
  application grading 1-4 (1s cancel, 2s→setter, 3-4s→closer; route best leads to best closers)
  and double-booking unconfirmed lower-grade leads. **A sub-70% *demo* show rate after a setter
  spoke to the lead is ~100% a setter skill issue** — QC the setter calls (best setters run
  80-90%). If everything's done and still sub-65%, it's an anomaly high-flake industry
  (contracting/biz-op, where 65% ≈ 80-90% in a sophisticated niche) or an offer/ad-congruency
  issue.
  → Lesson: E - Lead nurture & constraints — "Show rate best practices" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Flow before friction — and ~80% of "lead quality" problems are actually sales problems.** When
  launching, turn the lead fire-hose on (basic FB lead form, 1-2 questions, no-brainer claim + big
  guarantee) and bet on your own ability to deliver for the first 10-20 clients. Only *after*
  you're drowning do you add friction — gradually, one lever at a time up the friction ladder:
  (1) sharpen ad/funnel messaging (call out who it's for and NOT for) → (2) basic field
  requirements / phone verify → (3) one ICP hard-gate ("Do you own a [X]? if no, leave this
  page") → (4) lead form → landing page → (5) pixel conditioning → (6) VSL → (7) more qualifying
  questions → (8) aggressive financial DQ (rare). A qualified lead = in your market + buying
  authority + has the money + ready to solve now — and crucially qualification is NOT about how
  responsive they are (slow/flaky leads can be your highest-paying clients; the 11-location
  franchise no-showed his first THREE calls and became a top client). "Can't afford it" is usually
  a value-perception smokescreen (the $10k-Lamborghini test). Self-serving bias means closers
  blame "bad leads" rather than their selling — before declaring a quality problem, AUDIT the
  calls (is the closer folding on the first objection?). Quantitative triggers to add friction: DQ
  rate consistently >35%, or cost-per-qualified-call doubled from baseline; never act on 1-2 leads
  (~3-5 is a pattern). Cameron rarely DISQUALIFIES — he GRADES 1-4 and routes (never disqualify on
  revenue; auto-grade with a ChatGPT GHL action). Never change offer + qualifiers + ads + funnel
  all at once — you reset your data to zero.
  → Lesson: E - Lead nurture & constraints — "Lead quality best practices" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Pixel conditioning — the wrong-lead firewall (two thank-you pages).** After the first ~3-4
  conversion events the Meta pixel optimizes around those people, so bad early leads compound into
  more bad leads. Fix: add a "baked" qualifying question only a qualified lead answers correctly;
  if they answer the disqualifying way, route them to a DIFFERENT thank-you page that has NO pixel
  on it (identical otherwise — the only difference is one has the pixel, one doesn't). This stops
  unqualified people firing back to the pixel while you still keep them to sell/nurture. Optimize
  the ad set for "schedules," not "leads" (B2B); for B2C, optimize for "purchases"/deposits once
  you have data. Put the LEAD event on the schedule page and the SCHEDULE event on the thank-you
  page. (Cameron's agency runs a centralized pixel — one per client today, moving toward one per
  treatment bucket, all inside his own Business Manager and shared to clients; tested own vs shared
  over ~90 days and shared won, though he won't claim it's definitively the pixel vs market/luck.
  It also becomes a sales USP: "pixel with $10M ad spend / 100k patients generated." Protect your
  pixel — agencies "pixel-share" by joining each other's accounts; audit and remove shares.)
  → Founder call — "CSM management and service delivery" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]
  → Lesson: D - Funnels — "Funnel setup and optimization" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Competition hacking: model, don't rip off (the 70/20/10 rule).** Competitors have spent six
  figures discovering what works — use the Facebook Ads Library (+ Ads Library AI, vidIQ ~$20/mo
  for YouTube outlier scores, SpyFu for Google) to find ads running 7-14+ days AND/OR ≥$1,000
  spend (real "winner" signals), transcribe and extract the hook/pain/offer structure, re-script
  in your own words, film with your own face. Cross-pollinate from big spenders in OTHER niches
  (his best ideas came from operators doing $500k-$5M/mo elsewhere; "great artists steal" — model
  principles, never download-and-reupload). Don't model awareness/brand ads (Pepsi/Toyota run
  forever regardless of performance) — only direct-response offer ads that survive >2 weeks.
  Split: **70%** modeling what's proven (cash flow), **20%** winner-adjacent cross-pollinated
  angles, **10%** wild-card new mechanisms (80-90% of wildcards fail, but winners can't be copied,
  putting you 1-3 months ahead). Build competitor profiles + secret-shop competitors ~quarterly

## Decision rules
- If you can't spend ~$50/day for 60 days (~$3-5k you can lose) without pain → don't run ads yet;
  go all-in on ONE outbound strategy first (or run both, reinvest outbound cash into ads). <$50k
  bank → bootstrapped Path 2; >$50k and value time → fast Path 1.
- If ALL your metrics are broken (Layer-1 ROAS/CPA) → macro problem (offer/messaging); do NOT fix
  individual micro leaks. If only a Layer-2/3 metric is off but ROAS is healthy → it's an
  optimization, not a constraint; only chase it if ROAS is being capped.
- If multiple constraints → fix the highest-impact, easiest-to-implement, most-immediate one
  first (close rate before lead-to-booking). Juggling several is fine; being slow to implement is
  not.
- If you're cash-profitable with a consistent channel (UF cash ROAS >2.5x for 30+ days, net
  profit >30%, close >25%, show >70%, churn <15%) and availability is maxed → the only constraint
  is fuel: hire a closer, then "scale spend as much as humanly possible." Plan for "scale debt"
  (can your team onboard 20 new clients?).
- If availability-to-books ratio is ~85-95%+ → you are the bottleneck; hire/ramp a closer (add
  healthy friction in the meantime). Don't fall for "nobody can sell like me."
- If >2-3 leads/day and you're calling them yourself, OR >30% of demo calls are unqualified →
  hire a SETTER (pays for itself by closing one extra deal).
- If cash ROAS <1.0x while busy signing clients → pricing/profitability constraint; pull levers in
  order: raise price (month-to-month → 3-mo PIF for upfront cash; Cameron's UNVERIFIED rule of
  thumb is a 3-mo B2B program ≥ a few thousand, see Truth flags) → add back-end/commission LTV →
  fix lead-to-booking. Don't blame the ads.
- If profit margin is thin despite working ads → audit the P&L line by line; labor is usually
  bloated (overpaid friends, runaway commissions, premature onshore CMO/head-of-ops).
- If churn is consistently >15-20%/mo → fulfillment/client-success problem, not marketing; you
  can't outsell churn at scale (sometimes scale DOWN to fix the back end first).
- If an ad hasn't spent 3-5x your target KPI over 4-6 days → you have no data; don't judge it
  (small budgets stretch to 10-14 days, watch CTR/CPL). Early-kill only if ~$200 spent with CTR
  sub-1% and zero leads. Don't kill on 24h data.
- If a campaign is working → never raise its budget >~30% (10-30% in-campaign is fine); launch/
  duplicate a NEW campaign (same pixel) to scale. If it fell out of KPI after scaling → lower the
  budget 20-50% to force algorithmic selectivity.
- If you want more spend on one ad → duplicate it at higher budget; if it doesn't replicate, cut
  and re-duplicate up to ~5 times, then accept it and move to variation/audience scaling.
- If disqualifying a lot but front-end ROAS is profitable → scale it (squeeze the winner, 20% on
  higher-quality variations); don't panic over "bad lead quality."
- If CPL low but CPA huge (e.g. $20→$1000+), or >50% of leads unqualified, or the team spends >50%
  of time on unqualified calls → switch lead form → landing page (run in parallel at ~20% budget,
  min ~$100/day; judge on CPQL/show/close/CPA, not CPL).
- If your market is highly sophisticated (SaaS/agencies/ecom) → skip the lead-form start; open with
  a landing page + VSL day one.
- If unqualified leads start firing back to the pixel → pixel-condition: a baked qualifier routes
  DQ'd people to a no-pixel duplicate thank-you page; optimize the ad set for "schedules" (B2B) /
  "purchases" (B2C), not "leads."
- If a 2-call demo show rate is <70% after a setter spoke to the lead → it's a setter-skill issue
  (QC/coach, or set for a day yourself), not your ads/funnel.
- If show rate is sub-50% and you're overwhelmed → pull only the 3 immediate levers first (shorten
  booking window, call-confirm, fix thank-you page). If still <65% after all 15 → anomaly high-
  flake industry or offer/ad-congruency issue.
- If validating a NEW offer → create flow before any friction; bet on your own delivery for the
  first 10-20 clients; take the first 30-50 calls yourself; refund-and-learn beats perfectionism.
- If DQ rate on calls is consistently >35% OR cost-per-qualified-call has doubled → green light to
  add friction (one lever at a time, low → high). Never act on 1-2 leads.
- If close rate is <25% → assume sales skill (~80% of the time), not lead quality; audit the calls
  before touching marketing (don't take the closer's "bad leads" excuse).
- If show rate already 80%+ and close 30%+ and your only gap is lead flow → Hammer Them is a
  distraction; build it when scale degrades show/quality.
- If you find a winner → screenshot it, immediately make ~4 variations (RSA), change exactly ONE
  variable per test, never swap creative+copy+LP+offer at once.
- If you're capped on a high-end qualifier in a small geo → first switch images to 20-50 video
  creatives (usually lowers cost + raises quality); only if STILL capped go up-market, into the
  US, or horizontal to an adjacent niche reusing the same team.
- If you run a hiring/recruiting ad → put it on a SEPARATE ad account (a recruiting ad got banned
  and took down a founder's client ads); fix high cost-per-application with form-above-video +
  more LatAm countries.
- If on Instagram → use a DM-only CTA ("comment/DM [word]" → ManyChat flow), never a direct
  "book a call" link (open booking links pull unqualified bookings).
