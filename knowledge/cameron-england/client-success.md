# Cameron England — Client Success pillar (distilled)

## Use this card for
Keeping clients once they're sold: retention strategy, the call-and-comms cadence across the
client journey (onboarding → launch → check-in → reset → exit), handling pissed/cancelling
clients, increasing LTV via upsells/referral-partners/reviews, gathering social proof, building CS
systems that scale (CRM sub-account, ticketing, feedback surveys, churn audit, reactivation,
overdue-payment), and hiring/comping/managing/replacing a CSM team. Owns the question "why are
clients leaving and how do I make them stay?"

Does NOT cover: closing new business or the sales-call frame itself (→ sales pillar), the
inbound speed-to-lead / setter call-center booking machine (→ call-center pillar), or the actual
ad/fulfillment delivery that produces results (→ fulfillment-svd pillar). Lead generation lives
in the lead-gen pillar.

Never use this card for the user's own pricing/packaging/ICP/guarantee — those are mutable facts of their business, kept separate from doctrine.
Every dollar figure, comp number, and guarantee below is Cameron's agency (Pinpoint), not a template for the user.

## Core doctrine (distilled, evergreen)

- **Retention stands on two pillars — Results AND Relationship — and they're equal.** Most agencies
  obsess over results and "completely fuck up the relationship pillar." A strong relationship is the
  *insurance policy* for the inevitable bad month — when results dip, relationship is the only pillar
  the client can stand on. Weak relationship = the first results wobble = instant churn. The two leaks
  in any business are retention and sales; fix both and you grow.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "1. Client success - Start here" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **You cannot outsell a churn problem (the hamster-wheel).** Agencies fixate on the sexy front-end
  (the Stripe-screenshot dopamine of new cash); if clients walk out the back door as fast as you sign
  them, you burn out on a treadmill. Retention is the unsexy lever that makes the front-end matter.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "1. Client success - Start here" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Your CS team are salespeople (a second sales team), not support staff.** Cameron's single biggest
  mindset shift. The CSM is a coach/strategist/producer who **resells the client every interaction** —
  selling new strategies, upselling retainers/services, and selling them on referrals and reviews.
  Hunters (sales) kill new food; CSMs are farmers who cultivate land you already own. A reactive team
  that waits for complaints leaves money on the table. (The "X-on-the-slide" lesson: Cole Gordon's head
  of CS slapped Cameron for hiring on nurturing/always-available traits — the real win is coach +
  strategist + producer who'll hit projections.)
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "1. Client success - Start here" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: Live CSM calls — "Live group interview" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

- **Energy flows where focus goes — step on the scale; then get naked on it.** You can't improve what
  you don't measure; just tracking churn (like daily weigh-ins) creates unconscious behavior change.
  But count EVERY churn — pauses, vacations, "shouldn't-have-signed" clients — or you're a fat man
  weighing himself after a 72-hour fast and a sauna. A pause >1 month = churned. Churn = clients lost ÷
  clients at start of month, counted on the day they actually churn/don't renew.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "2. Master CS tracking dashboard" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Overcommunicate and centralize — "the left hand talks to the right hand."** Log every key
  touchpoint in ONE source of truth (Monday/ClickUp). At scale the media buyer changes something the
  CSM doesn't know → embarrassing on-call moments and churn. Spreadsheets work at ~5 clients but break
  past ~20. The runaway-CSM "Cat" story (first CSM left with no notice at ~20-30 clients/$50-60k/mo,
  everything in a messy Google sheet → Cameron had no idea how clients were doing) is *why* he moved to
  a PM platform. Monday must stay current on touchpoints, DEFCON, last-TP date, recordings — "if the
  CSM gets hit by a bus" the team can still run every client.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "3. Client management overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Texting/Slack = "the glue between the calls."** Calls are the pillars; day-to-day text is the glue
  — "without the glue the whole journey goes to shit." Biweekly calls alone are too long a feedback
  loop; the messaging layer is the actual key to retention. Airport-delay analogy: update the board
  every 30-60 min even with no news, because people would rather know too much than too little.
  → Lesson: Communication — "1. Call types breakdown" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Be proactive, never reactive — "know the problem before the client knows it's a problem."** Almost
  every fire traces to the CSM learning of a problem at the same moment the client did. Clients tolerate
  slow results; they will not tolerate having to nag you and feeling things only improve when they
  complain. Every system (notifications, stat sheets, hot list, monthly projections) exists to know
  before the client knows. Go into every call already holding the constraint and the fix.
  → Lesson: Communication — "5. Client check in framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Match communication to the client's "DNA" — be a communication chameleon.** Two types cover ~90%
  (and ~80% of clients are emotional, not analytical). **Analytical/driver**: data, logic, no fluff,
  challenges anything illogical — cut small talk, lead with numbers, block less time when performance is
  fine. **Emotional**: operates on feeling, needs reassurance, overreacts to single events (usually
  newer owners who can't regulate stress) — small talk, share every small win, contact more often, keep
  data high-level. People default to communicating the way *they* like — the hammer-for-every-task
  mistake. "People buy from people like them; people stay with people like them."
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "4. Client personalities" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The emotional-client rule: maintain frame, do the OPPOSITE of the panic, let the "monkey brain"
  vent then validate with logic.** Under stress the irrational monkey brain reacts first. Let it rant,
  ask permission before correcting ("do you mind if I share my thoughts based on my marketing
  experience?"), acknowledge feelings, then validate with high-level data, reassure constantly.
  Proof-of-method: a CSM who matched a combative client's energy and argued back → instant churn;
  Cameron got on after, stayed logical, validated, let them vent → resold them. The issue is HOW you
  say it, not WHAT. The one-no-show overreaction (client sees 1 no-show of 10, demands upfront-only) →
  pull the data, show show-rate beats the 75% KPI, normalize ("tires go flat, kids get sick"), refuse
  the irrational change. For emotional clients: note birthdays/kids' games in the PM tool, follow them
  on social, send handwritten cards/books.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "4. Client personalities" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Dangle the carrot — always keep something new on the horizon.** Frame future improvements as done
  *for this client specifically* and tied to a concern they previously stated ("you mentioned X, so I
  took it to the team and we're now doing Y for you"). Even a generic hire reframes as "I onboarded a
  funnel specialist building YOU a custom funnel in 24 hours." Creates a "just one more month" mentality
  that holds clients with flat results — some stayed up to a year on poor results. Do it at the END of a
  check-in or 1-to-1 DM, never a mass announcement (he tried; far weaker). Cadence every 2-4 weeks, more
  for near-churn clients. His lowest-ever churn month (0%, ~50 clients, 2 months) was caused by moving
  the call center offshore→onshore — which he realized afterward was a *dangled carrot* he didn't know
  he was using. Dangle the carrot to your TEAM too — people don't stay where they don't feel they'll grow.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "5. Dangling the carrot" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The five-call client journey + the glue.** Sales call (rep collects payment) → Onboarding → Launch
  → biweekly Check-ins → (Reset/Exit when needed), all bound by Slack. The documented version lists, per
  step, the assigned person, what can go wrong, the fix, and a difficulty level — built so a new CSM
  knows what "success" looks like at each milestone. The check-in is the main management tool and where
  most upsells happen (no separate "upsell call").
  → Lesson: Communication — "1. Call types breakdown" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Onboarding & launch are "re-sales" calls — they bridge sales→CS and reinforce the buying decision.**
  Reps oversell; the CSM realigns expectations DOWN to realistic and re-sells "we're the best to execute
  this." People decide within ~48 hours of buying whether they'd buy again (Hormozi's CLOSER "R") — so
  roll out the red carpet in the first 24-48h. Sales→CSM handoff: closer drops the sales-call transcript
  into a form, ChatGPT generates the CSM's briefing bullets so they don't walk in blind ("I've already
  connected with David and gone over your constraints"). The onboarding call's whole job is the bridge —
  the client should walk over it, not have to swim.
  → Lesson: Communication — "1. Call types breakdown" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The onboarding-call order (the 80/20): rapport → set the frame → audit the client profile FIRST,
  THEN walk resources.** The #1 mistake is walking the presentation/trainings before auditing the
  profile/goals — you have no North Star to tie anything back to, and that "unravels the entire call."
  Set the frame at the open: agenda (get to know you & why you joined; housekeeping; map the next 90
  days) + a ~60-min time box + "we're taking notes for you so you can be present." Build the client
  profile: reconfirm the revenue goal, label the problem, capture monetary AND non-monetary "why" — this
  is the ammunition you revert to when they're close to churning. 10 steps: profile/goals → agreement →
  onboarding info (EIN for A2P) → Slack onboarding live → Facebook access → CRM + calendar → offer
  foundations → content → sales training → chat widget + BAMFAM the launch call. Whiz through low-value
  housekeeping (give the dopamine of a completed form); slow down and tie down on the OFFER and sales.
  → Lesson: Communication — "3. Onboarding call framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: Live client calls — "3. Onboarding - Sophisticated client" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The launch call is the #1 churn-prevention lever — a deck, not a script, and a living document.**
  Held ~24h before going live, ~30-40 min. Four objectives: set crystal-clear expectations, show
  tangible assets ("show, don't tell" — screen-share the real ads/funnel/script), excite, iron out
  confusion BEFORE spending their money. Every churn → audit the sales call then the launch call: what
  expectation did I fail to set? Add a slide. Cameron's launch deck "changes every month minimum."
  Named churn-driven slides he added: the Google-calendar block-out (a client blocked their calendar
  wrong → double-booked → furious → churned), turn off the client's own appointment reminders
  (double-confirmation churn), "quality of your submissions dictates quality of your results" (the
  smoke-screen stat-sheet), and the sales-training slide (lead-quality complaints were really
  warm-referral sellers meeting cold leads). Set expectations LOW: "month one is mainly building, no
  leads week 1-2 is normal." If a client moans 1-3 months in about something they were warned about,
  he literally plays the recorded launch call back ("I told you this would be an issue, John" — always
  record on Fathom).
  → Lesson: Communication — "4. Launch call and process framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Stat sheet = the client's #1 job and the single most important retention lever ("what isn't
  tracked can't be improved").** ~1 hour after every appointment, GHL sends the client a status form
  (show/no-show, lead quality, 2nd appt booked, $ spent, real objection) that auto-feeds the master
  stat sheet. Remove friction: hidden fields auto-attribute the lead, so name/email/phone fields are
  cut. Reject "needs to think" as a close reason — it's a smokescreen; demand the REAL reason (price?
  declined financing?). Decisions come from two data sources: the fulfillment team's reports + the
  client's stat sheet. "Driving on I-thinks not I-knows is driving blindfolded — you end dead in a
  ditch."
  → Lesson: Communication — "4. Launch call and process framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **BAMFAM — Book A Meeting From A Meeting.** Never end a call without the next touchpoint on the
  calendar, confirmed live (don't say "I'll send a link"). Immediately after launch, BAMFAM the
  7-day "iron out the kinks" call (tech + expectation issues are *expected* ~7-10 days in) and a
  ~21-day system audit, then biweekly. Bi-weekly beats weekly (~2x team efficiency, no churn impact,
  and weekly conditions clients to expect week-over-week miracles). After 90 days, stable clients go
  on-demand; near-churn clients tighten to weekly.
  → Lesson: Communication — "1. Call types breakdown" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Run check-ins on pre/on/post and "address the elephant" FIRST.** Pre (5-15 min): walk the
  four-reasons ladder on the fulfillment sheet to isolate the one broken stage, line up the fix already
  in motion, wins/cookies, an opportunity (upsell/referral/review), and a carrot; check if the stat
  sheet is updated. On: rapport → set the frame + YOU raise the bad number before they do ("let me shoot
  the elephant in the room — I know we're not getting many appointments, the team came up with ideas,
  here they are… before we dive in, anything you want to add?") → break the elephant down (what / what
  we're doing now / progress / next steps) → stats (call out an empty stat sheet with the *confused*
  frame, then reset expectations hard) → wins → opportunities (just ask) → homework with urgency + due
  date + a why → BAMFAM. Post: notify fulfillment in Slack, then fill the post-call summary FORM (not
  just Monday notes) — a Make+ChatGPT scenario summarizes action items, runs an AI sales-audit grading
  the call 1-10 (red/orange/green), updates Monday (recording, to-dos, DEFCON, last-TP), assigns the
  task, and Slacks the client a pinned summary.
  → Lesson: Communication — "5. Client check in framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: CS systems — "4. Client call tasks automation" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Diagnose the constraint by the funnel before every check-in (the 4 reasons / the "gap").** Only
  four reasons a client gets no results: not enough LEADS (CPL), leads not converting to BOOKINGS
  (schedule rate), bookings not SHOWING (show rate), shows not CLOSING (close rate / cash collected).
  Whack-a-mole: there is never zero constraint — find the one binding stage, pull the matching lever,
  another pops. Crucially, the further down the funnel toward the close, the more control the CLIENT has
  and the less you do — so the ladder also tells you whose problem it is. Watch for false reads: a high
  cost-per-show with good CPL/cost-per-booking usually means the stat sheet isn't being filled, not a
  real problem.
  → Lesson: Communication — "5. Client check in framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Client no-show protocol.** ~5 min after: "I'm on, excited to catch up — you hopping on?" (+ a
  smiling selfie). ~5 more: "you're usually super punctual… hopping off, I'll shoot a Loom." Then send a
  Loom breaking down their stats. 2-4 no-shows in a row → re-level-set commitment and offer to switch to
  async (video breakdowns + Slack). For flaky blue-collar niches, just ring them 1-3x/week — they
  no-show scheduled calls.
  → Lesson: Communication — "5. Client check in framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Slack setup: two channels, two workspaces, faceless departments, Slack sprints.** Per client:
  `name-general` (CSM↔client) + `name-scheduling` (booking/call-center) so notifications don't bury real
  talk. Two workspaces (internal + a free external one). **Identity-mask** every non-CSM team member in
  the external Slack — agency logo + their *position* as name ("Media Team") — so a departing person a
  client bonded with doesn't trigger churn (a real call-center agent left → client churned), and the
  client isn't overwhelmed by many faces; the CSM is the only personal identity. **Slack sprints**:
  3× 15-min windows/day to clear unreads — stop replying within 2 min, which trains whining = instant
  babysitting. Push media/tech/call-center to message the client directly (the full team is a real
  selling point, "not an illusion"). Known risk: in a shared workspace clients can see each other's
  channel names/member counts and DM each other — restrict via Slack Connect/guest setup. If a client
  publicly bashes a setter in the channel, delete the message and handle it privately.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "3. Client management overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: Communication — "2. Client communication SOP" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Templatize day-to-day comms, but as guardrails not handcuffs.** A living template directory for
  every scenario (just-launched, no-call-2-weeks, performance-bad-new-client, review/referral asks, tech
  & media VA templates) so a CSM copies instead of writing for 15 min. 10 rotating general-check-in
  variations so a client never gets the identical message twice. "Send / send / send" = separate
  messages, never one block (a block reads spammy/templated). The doc compounds — add "client cries on
  call → send this," then Cmd-F "cries" later. Move all email→branded templates (Cameron funnel-hacked a
  SaaS competitor's branded emails — small thing, big lift). Three Slack routing rules: basic question →
  tag the department; complex/touchy → send a Loom (conveys tonality text can't); concern/emotional →
  DEFCON-1 → call ASAP. Keep the CSM off low-leverage admin (rescheduling, availability) — their time is
  the four R's and calls.
  → Lesson: Communication — "2. Client communication SOP" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Pissed clients = an expectation gap, handled stoically (the glass case).** Visualize three lines:
  oversold (where they arrived), realistic (middle), below-realistic (bottom). A *negative* gap (you had
  to drag expectations down) always produces a pissed client; underset-and-overdeliver so realistic
  lands as a win (promise 5, deliver 15 → thrilled; promise 30, deliver 20 → frustrated). They're rarely
  angry at *you* — their perceived reality ≠ actual reality. Imagine a glass case so nothing they say
  "hits" you; get triggered and you turn a dance into a fight you lose (they hold the chargeback).
  → Lesson: Client conflict — "1. Pissed client scenarios" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Get in the angry boat (extreme ownership) — the 7-step pissed-client process.** There can only be
  one person in the angry boat — make it you, *angrier at your own team/situation than the client is*;
  then they can't stay angry at you. Steps: (1) pre-call prep — black-and-white proof (the launch
  recording, screenshots, stat sheet), diagnose the constraint, arrive WITH 1-2 solutions; (2) set the
  frame / take control — name the problems yourself, never open passively ("I heard you had questions");
  (3) let them fully vent, collect the *complete* list before solving any; (4) Acknowledge-&-Clarify
  per problem — validate, then *over-state* the problem back ("the scheduling mistake, 100% on us,
  screwed your calendar, probably cost you thousands, you're wondering why you still work with us — is
  that about right?") until they pull back ("well, it's not that bad" = you're now allied); (5) ASK
  PERMISSION before sharing your side; (6) solutions FIRST, proof only if their complaint contradicts
  evidence (ask before pulling up the recording); (7) realign + ASSUME continuation — never ask "do you
  still want to continue?" (it's like asking "do you want to pay now?"). Feeling understood > being
  agreed with. The botched-call anti-pattern: rep had camera off, didn't set the frame, defended on the
  first concern, never took ownership without proof, kept defending a $67 cost-per-booking the client
  didn't care about, then just handed back $2,266. (Cameron credits the angry-boat to Hormozi.)
  → Lesson: Client conflict — "1. Pissed client scenarios" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: Live client calls — "Refund request angry call audit" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Three conflict scenarios → three different calls.** Refund OR cancel demand → **Exit call** (save or
  harvest brutal feedback). Misaligned/disrespectful/scope-creeping/late-paying → **Reset call** (realign
  hard boundaries or part ways). You/your team screwed up → **Ownership call** (take the hit in the first
  30s). An ownership call whose plan is rejected transitions into the exit framework.
  → Lesson: Client conflict — "1. Pissed client scenarios" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Exit call: speed-to-lead on churn intent, then save or harvest feedback.** The moment a client
  signals cancel intent = DEFCON-1: call within the hour, push for a Zoom ASAP; the exit call is the
  company's #1-priority slot (move everything else), ideally within 24h. Contract clause: a mandatory
  5-10 min exit call + cancellation survey before anyone can cancel (works as a bluff ~9.5/10 times).
  Reset your own state for 10-20 min before (go outside / work out) — the calmest person controls the
  call. In-call: set the frame to remove the "you're forcing me to stay" feeling ("if by the end I
  genuinely feel not working together is best for you, I'll shoot it straight — I just want you to leave
  with a game plan"), let them vent stoically, restate → solution per concern, check completeness, then
  if still dead-set run the **Socratic exit-plan dismantle** (never tell a dead-set client their plan
  won't work — ask questions until *they* conclude it: the events teardown — "one event, $10k, $3k cost,
  once a month, mostly existing clients… events are the cherry, not the cake; you can't put $1 in and get
  $5 out reliably"). Then the **churn ladder**: same price (fix problems) → 50% off next month →
  2-week extension → 4-week extension → churn. Prefer an extension over a refund; prefer working free
  over a cash refund. Keep the bridge open (Slack access + offer future support) — never burn it.
  → Lesson: Client conflict — "2. Exit process and call framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Push hard on the save — but know the stop point.** Cameron explicitly prefers a CSM push too hard
  than too little ("better to hold the lead too much than not at all"); you can save ~50-60% (7-8 of 10)
  of would-be losses on these calls. BUT once the client has clearly, repeatedly said no, STOP — "you
  emptied the gun, then tried to hit her on the head with it." Over-pushing past the no burns the lead
  and risks bad reviews; pivot to a smooth off-board. Tie the save back to the client's actual #1
  problem/values, not your logic (a CSM lost an exit call arguing Meta vs Google when the client's real
  pain was brand/reputation alignment). On the failed-reframe: never minimize their gap ("we're only 10%
  apart" when they feel 90% apart) — treat their problem as even bigger than they do; if you care more
  than they do, they trust you with the small things.
  → Lesson: Live CSM calls — "Exit call audit - pushing hard" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

- **Reset call: realign hard boundaries or part ways cleanly.** Prepare a written boundary list before
  (after-hours messaging, disrespect, constant direction changes, removing you from ad manager).
  In-call: set the frame ("expectations feel misaligned, that's okay — what's not okay is not addressing
  it; let's figure out if we realign or part ways at month-end"), let them vent and accept legit blame,
  then run YOUR concerns down the list, each as a boundary with a reason ("we can't keep changing
  direction — the moment a campaign gets traction you switch and we rebuild from zero"; "our quality
  needs comms in Slack — email/text is fine but slower"). Confirm alignment, then decide. One more reset
  if a boundary breaks again, then exit. Always finish the paid month respectfully; be ready for a refund
  request after letting someone go.
  → Lesson: Client conflict — "3. Reset call framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Ownership call: take the hit in 30 seconds, fix + prevent, compensate last.** Initiate it the
  moment YOU find the mistake (don't wait to be caught). Book it instantly ("me and the team completely
  messed up — want to go over what we're doing to fix it and ensure it never happens again — what time
  works?"). In-call, no rapport-building: take 100% ownership up front, get angrier than them at your own
  team (swear if needed: "I've already given that media buyer a final warning… if I were you I'd be
  pissed, nothing excuses this"), present **Solution + Prevention** (the prevention — a new SOP/approval
  step — is what earns confidence to stay), then offer compensation only if warranted, then assume the
  forward plan. Own it regardless of who erred. The overspend example: new ad account, media buyer
  didn't kill the old ads → overspend → solution = turned off + credited the exact amount; prevention =
  SOP requiring media buyers to double-check all prior ad accounts. "It takes 37 magical moments to
  overcome one tragic moment" (Disney) — a refund alone makes it "a little okay," not okay; don't lead
  with compensation (treat it like a guarantee — offering it unnecessarily just loses money).
  → Lesson: Client conflict — "4. Ownership call framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Refund handling: anchor to the guarantee doc on screen, offer value not cash, assess the
  nut-job radar.** Pull up the actual contract/recording ("I don't want you thinking I made this up").
  A work-free guarantee can't be triggered by a 30-day result if the window is 90 days; deliverables
  already rendered aren't refundable. Offer alternatives (work free until the deliverable; free DIY-program
  access) over cash. Assess reputation-damage likelihood: if high (will spam Trustpilot / comment on
  ads), just refund; if low (<~40%), play ballsy with proof, only refunding if they threaten a
  chargeback. For chargebacks, flood Stripe with the contract + sales-call recording + the "not fully
  refundable" checkbox screenshot (you still win only ~50% — avoid Fanbasis for disputes). A sub-5%
  refund rate is fine; being too soft on refunds also harms the client (they assume they'll recoup it)
  and undermines your CSMs/closers.
  → Lesson: Live client calls — "Refund request prevention with proof" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]
  → Lesson: CS systems — "5. Giving clients extensions" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Churn is feedback — a free feedback center; keep the bridge open.** Study every churn like game
  tape after a loss; a leaving client gives more honest feedback than a happy one. The live Dr. Walker
  exit call (~$7,700 client) yielded "gold": call-center turnover → he built a round-robin call center
  with cheat sheets; "working in the dark" not knowing which ad a lead saw → he instituted the launch
  call walking clients through all assets; un-archived creatives → a centralized media directory sorted
  best→worst. "The highest-ROI 20-30 minutes of my life." Run exit calls RAW, not scripted — better
  feedback when it feels raw; visibly take notes and restate. Don't delete churned clients: contract the
  exit-call requirement, capture structured reasons, segment Lost Good Terms vs Lost Bad Terms (nut-job
  or irreparable mess-up = no reactivation), and nurture the rest. ~10% of churned clients reactivate
  monthly at ~$0 CAC; 20-30-40% come back when the grass isn't greener ("the grass is greener where you
  water it"). The franchise that came back replied to a nurture text 3 months later and signaled ~5
  locations.
  → Lesson: Client conflict — "5. Getting feedback from clients" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: CS systems — "2. Offboarding and reactivating clients" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **The 4 R's = the LTV / money-making levers; ~90% of a CSM's time goes here, ≤10% on admin.** Retain,
  Resell (upsell), Refer, Review. Track every live opportunity on a daily-updated **hot list** (one
  pinned Google Sheet: client, temperature [red-hot = closing in days / hot = this week / on-hold],
  opportunity type, last objection, amount, last + next follow-up, notes) — most of the 4 R's close in
  the follow-up, not the first ask. Leadership-based selling: "tell them what they need to hear, not what
  they want" — an upsell is the natural consequence of a client's own stated ambition, not a tactic.
  → Lesson: Upsells/referral-partners/reviews — "1. Money making levers" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Upsells: the spend-gap model + four upsell categories + closing mechanics.** Four categories:
  additional locations (≈doubles LTV), additional campaigns/ad spend, additional services (SEO, chat
  widget, reactivation), and the backend/continuity program. The **spend gap**: the retainer is fixed,
  so as a client increases ad spend the gap between what they spend and make widens — higher ROI, longer
  retention. Offer "the fast way" (double spend → ~1.8x return) vs "the slow way" (5-15% via
  optimization). The tough-love script: client at 30k vs a 50k goal — "I need to tell you what you need
  to hear… I want you to *double* your ad spend" (tell, don't ask), pause through the objection, "$1 in →
  $5 out." Verify capacity before scaling (can the clinic handle 30-40 more patients?); above ~$100/day,
  every +~$3k/mo spend = +$500/mo retainer OR deposit-collection to align incentives — confirm BEFORE
  launching. Closing non-negotiables: confirm charging the card-on-file ON the call ("does the card on
  file work?"); tell don't ask; don't drop to payment plans too early. The chat-widget upsell: pitch it
  even if they already have one — "the problem isn't the widget, it's who's on the back end of it" (the
  founder who never replies). SaaS billing-cycle psychology: the more often you bill, the more they think
  about paying, the higher the churn — stretch to 6-/12-month prepay via continuity.
  → Lesson: Upsells/referral-partners/reviews — "2. Upsells" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: Live client calls — "4.Onboarding upsell" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

- **Backend/continuity renewal: run it as a normal check-in, NEVER rename it.** The renewal/ascension
  is "offboarding," framed 1-2 weeks before the program ends (a PM task auto-fires at 14 days) but it
  must look like a normal biweekly check-in — relabeling it ("upsell call," "ascension call") telegraphs
  the pitch. Order: **Wins → Goals → Transition → Pitch.** Bonus-stack: give everything they had PLUS
  extras (a founder sales intensive, a free month of SEO, an included campaign, a free month with an
  additional location) so they're "paying for continuity at a cheaper price" plus a sweetener. The last
  10-14 days of a 90-day program = heavy proactive warm-up touchpoints. Back-end programs are the
  highest-leverage, hardest-to-delegate sale — the founder should keep selling them personally as long
  as possible ("scale the unscalable").
  → Lesson: Upsells/referral-partners/reviews — "1. Money making levers" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Referrals: ask confrontationally at the PEAK state, on a call, with a direct intro.** The first ask
  is a dedicated slide at the END of the launch call ("some clinics make a full-time income just
  referring us"); after that, ask at activation points / right after a win — never on a fixed timeline
  and never desperate at onboarding (franchise owners often guard "the best marketing company" and won't
  refer regardless). Ask "Who comes to mind that we can reach out to?" — never leave it open ("let me
  know if someone comes to mind"). Goal: get contact info, ideally a live group chat (WhatsApp/iMessage)
  with an intro + a smiling-Zoom-screenshot for congruency. Channel order: Zoom > phone > voice note >
  text. The CSM hands the actual sale to the sales team. Referred clients stay longest.
  → Lesson: Upsells/referral-partners/reviews — "3. Refferals" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Social proof is a flywheel and a moat — gather at the win, then squeeze the juice.** Hierarchy
  (best→worst): client interview (20-40 min Q&A) > video testimonial (1-5 min) > public reviews
  (Trustpilot/Google) > win screenshots. ~99% of agencies have no public review page (scared of bad
  reviews) — having one is a sales weapon. Ask at the win (dessert/steak analogy: ask straight after the
  steak, not before and not 5 hours later); request channel Zoom > phone > voice note > Loom > text
  (reviews can just be a voice note — far fewer objections than an upsell). Use a passive,
  permission-based frame ("would you be open? I won't send anything without your permission") then send
  the link — reciprocity raises the yes. Four steps: ask → stretch the yes into a full interview ("we're
  building the content side, 10-20 min for YouTube, could grow your brand too") → conduct it → "squeeze
  the juice." Interview structure mirrors a sales call in PAST tense, internal + external at each state
  to elicit emotion: intro → past problem → past goal → what they love (freestyle) → "what would you say
  to someone on the fence?" (pre-handles objections). Distribute everywhere: YouTube → opt-in page (keep
  simple, ~2) → thank-you page (overwhelm them) → a single dedicated results page (one link a prospect
  can't say no to) → ad hooks/retargeting. Match the testimonial avatar to the prospect ("people buy
  from people like them" — the Bronx client closed by a same-profile testimonial). Build a case-study
  doc per avatar the sales team sends post-demo. Negative reviews: stay stoic, sleep on it, try admin
  removal (~48h, ~50/50), then direct outreach to remove, then a measured public reply — or just pay a
  fake/competitor reviewer ~$500 to remove rather than tit-for-tat. A few bad reviews among many add
  authenticity ("it looks fake when you have none"). Build the public page only at churn <~10-12%; hold
  off above ~18% (Cameron launched Trustpilot too early and regretted the bad reviews).
  → Lesson: Upsells/referral-partners/reviews — "4. Gathering social proof" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Anonymous feedback beats polite answers — NPS + CSAT at activation points (the Emirates system).**
  Clients won't be honest to your face then cancel two weeks later (the breakup "it's not you, it's me"
  problem). NPS = anonymous, recurring, overall pulse (1-10 recommend + rate comms + what to add/remove);
  CSAT = NOT anonymous, fired right after a specific event (you need who/what context). The Emirates
  epiphany: surveys land best right after consumption, at the activation point. Place CSAT ~1h after the
  onboarding call and at cancellation; place NPS ~1h after onboarding, then month 1 / 3 / 6 (because
  macro churn is too noisy week-to-week). Send the reminder from a PERSONAL account, on the 1st or 28th
  (not 30th/31st — short months bug out). Act only on PATTERNS (3-5+ clients saying the same = a fact,
  not an opinion); ignore single contradictory opinions (personality types). "Be willing to get punched
  in the face by the truth." Feed CSAT/NPS into the churn-audit agent.
  → Lesson: CS systems — "8. Client feedback surverys" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Systematize to scale: client sub-account, ticketing, overdue-payment, extensions, reactivation.**
  Keep a SEPARATE CRM sub-account for clients vs pre-sale leads (split once you sign ~10/mo) — the
  firewall protects sales-rep conviction (reps who see angry client messages close worse). The client
  sub-account is an automation/lifecycle hub (onboarding → live → lost), NOT a notes tool (that's
  Monday). The **ticketing system** is the "central nervous system" — every common request = a ticket →
  form → auto-creates a PM task → routes to the right department → attaches the SOP → sets a deadline →
  posts on the client board (each ticket carries the client ID — left hand talks to right hand). The
  3-plus rule: don't build a giant library up front; SOP-ify a task once it recurs 2-3 times. The
  flagship "small crack" example: a client changes an offer name, the media buyer updates ads + landing
  page but forgets the call-center script → leads think it's a scam → churn ("death by a thousand cuts").
  Ticketing is for repeatable tasks only — complex one-offs stay outside (or you make your team "mindless
  zombies"). **Overdue payments**: the BILLING VA chases, never the CSM (good-cop/bad-cop — the moment a
  CSM asks for money the relationship turns transactional); break a failed full payment into
  halves/quarters first; unresponsive after 3 days → run the offboarding SOP. **Extensions**: eligible
  if the failure is YOUR fault (bad leads, poor show rate, overspend), NOT if it's the client's (not
  closing, not filling the stat sheet, no-showing) — gray areas still get one as a good-faith gesture;
  target extension rate <5%, extension-renewal ≥50%; if an uncontrollable reason recurs, add it as a
  launch-call expectation. **Use the client ID (not name/email) as the lookup key everywhere** — IDs
  don't change and have ~zero typo risk.
  → Lesson: CS systems — "6. Client ticketing systems" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: CS systems — "1. Client Sub Account" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: CS systems — "3. Overdue payment and ghosting" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Reactivation: keep the bridge open and run a sales-call win-back at $0 CAC.** Reactivation nurture
  ~90 days (touches day 1/30/45/90, text + email); on a positive reply, notify the CSM/rep with the
  strongest relationship + the reactivation script. Win-back call: Set & frame ("saw you responding…
  we've been ironing out the kinks since we worked together") → Discovery → Updated positioning (name
  the exact reason they churned, then "that's exactly what we've rebuilt our process to solve") → CTA
  (30-day trial) → downsell ladder (more lenient since CAC = $0: paid-in-full → split-pay → down payment
  → 30-day test drive; lean on test drives, over-delivering 30 days costs nothing). Send a "goodbye
  packet" (recap, handover, access-removal, "if you wanted to work with us again…", testimonials).
  Monthly churn ritual: export the cancellation tracker → the pre-built churn-audit AI agent (+ niche +
  ICP) → top churn themes, fixable vs acceptable loss, priority fixes → review with your most-trusted
  people.
  → Lesson: CS systems — "2. Offboarding and reactivating clients" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **DEFCON client-health triage + the management board.** Each client carries a DEFCON flag (1 =
  urgent/close to churn, 3 = performing well — the team repeatedly confuses the direction; what matters
  is one triage flag so you instantly see who needs attention) plus separate pause/churn states. The
  management board prioritizes attention in four sections: First 90 days / Renewed / Paused / Lost —
  first-90-day clients sit at the top because they're the highest churn risk (auto-flagged "high
  priority" for 30 days, auto-demoted after). A "last touchpoint" view buckets clients (Urgent 1-month+,
  2-weeks+, 7-14 days). Target: client live within 7-10 days of signing.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "3. Client management overview" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Weekly client-planning ritual (the CS Monday meeting).** Each CSM copies the template weekly and
  fills: clients due for offboarding/renewal (with % likelihood + the renew angle), DEFCON-1 clients +
  recovery plan, referral/review/upsell projections, and clients untouched 2+ weeks. Reviewed Monday;
  projections are compared to last week's actuals ("you said 3 referrals — how many did you get?") and
  monthly four-R targets break down to weekly → daily → specific-client. Never answer "let me check" on
  a CS call — the doc means you've already mapped who's at risk.
  → Lesson: Communication — "6. Weekly client planning process" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Hire a CSM to buy back time — but learn the role first and don't rush it.** Hire around 10-15
  clients (with cash flow) at ~75-80% capacity — handing off 10 is far easier than 30+. Delegate to
  elevate; treat attention like a lighthouse beam (spread across everything = weak everywhere). Don't
  hire before your CS process exists — you'll just pay someone to manage chaos; minimum bar is you can
  run every call yourself with ~5 gold-standard recordings to train on (exception: a 2nd business where
  you already have CS experience → delegate ASAP). Screen hard (the stickiest, most dangerous-to-lose
  hire — clients attach loyalty to the CSM, not the agency): job post → application → group interview
  (~60% certainty to advance) → 1:1 → test project → 3-5 reference checks (also LinkedIn colleagues) →
  reverse-selling interview ("interviewing is two people professionally lying for an hour" — tell them
  every reason NOT to join). Test project with a deliberate trap (an invalid call planted among examples
  to test attention to detail) + delivering bad news to a non-performer; the winning candidate diagnosed
  Oct/Nov stats unprompted (a "killer") and replied in ~15 min vs 15 hrs.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "6. Everything on hiring a CSM" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: CSM management — "2. Auditing CSM's live" · [COURSE-MEDIUM]

- **The A-player CSM archetype = the burnt-out sales rep; coachability/traits > experience.** Both of
  Cameron's best, longest-tenured CSMs are ex-sales reps (sales skill + now want a base — often after a
  kid/illness; their willingness to have taken commission-only roles proves they bet on themselves).
  Kindness + competence (warm enough to build rapport, authoritative enough to tell a client "you might
  be wrong"). Avoid pure-corporate customer-service backgrounds (entitlement, want everything
  structured, no tolerance for startup chaos — hiring those was one of his named mistakes; a
  pure-corporate CSM out-argued him in a 1:1, correctly noting he'd hired the wrong profile AND didn't
  yet know how to manage managers). "Wallflop" tell: if a candidate dodges a direct question with a
  tangent, don't hire. Hire ONSHORE in the client's country for lingo (one extra retained client/month
  pays the onshore premium). Source: Indeed (#1) → referrals → LinkedIn → recruiters.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "6. Everything on hiring a CSM" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: Live CSM calls — "Auditing uncomfortable 1-1 firing call" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Onboard a CSM like a client (Clarity / Resources / Support) — bite-sized, in client-journey order.**
  You're in the talent-acquisition game more than the client-acquisition game (ramping 2-3 CSMs is
  harder than signing 30 clients). Three pillars for any hire: Clarity (the scorecard — show what success
  looks like), Resources (tools + all SOPs = the "boot camp"), Support (1:1s, team meetings, access). If
  you do all three and they still fail, it's a "them" problem — but most people don't do all three.
  Don't dump all training at once (Cameron had 2-3 CSMs churn in 30 days from overwhelm); learn ONE
  call/topic → quiz/assessment → implement live, in the order clients experience the calls. The bare
  minimum bootcamp: scorecard video + commission video + the client journey A-to-Z + a breakdown of each
  call with ≥3 example recordings (bonus: you upselling) + a text-communication video. Per-call ramp
  loop: assign videos + homework → assessment/roleplay (you play the client) → first live call → audit
  it after (Cameron prefers auditing after vs live shadowing — takes the pressure off). Hand off the
  EASIEST existing clients first (2x2: most-pain-but-easiest-to-delegate first) to buy back time fastest.
  3-plus rule: same question/mistake 3x → make a video/SOP. Static vs Dynamic vs Depends content (reuse
  static as-is, rebuild dynamic — updated ~2-3x/year). The 1-3-1 rule: past ramp, a CSM brings 1 problem
  + 3 solutions + 1 recommendation, never a bare problem. Ramp two CSMs together (cohort) so they bounce
  off each other.
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "7.CSM Onboarding and bootcamp" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: CSM management — "1. How to handoff clients to a CSM" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: Live CSM calls — "Onboarding Call with CSM" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Comp a CSM to align across all four R's — never on churn alone or base alone.** Cameron's preferred
  model = base + commission (upsell %, referral/review bonuses, tiered retention bonus, optional capacity
  bonus per client past a cap). Churn-only comp makes them cap their client load low; base-only breeds
  complacency. The base-floor + per-client-over-threshold trick (base for managing up to N clients, then
  $50-100 per additional retained client) makes them get creative about capacity instead of hating new
  signups. Avoid commissioning on data the CLIENT controls (paying on "showed appointments" backfired —
  reps spammed clients to update stat sheets). Two-projection system (Blood = bare minimum / Stretch =
  what pushes them, must require proactive effort); hit stretch ~70-80% of the time. (Specific dollar
  figures are Cameron's agency — UNVERIFIED.)
  → Lesson: Fundamentals — "6. Everything on hiring a CSM" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: CSM management — "3. Daily CSM meetings and framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Manage the CS team on a cadence: weekly planning + daily dev meeting + 1:1s + game tape + EODs.**
  Meet the CSM team daily if bandwidth allows (sales + CS are the two highest-leverage points); if
  running everything yourself, 3-4/week is fine. Cameron's weekly cadence: Mon = DEFCON/planning, Tue
  off, Wed = roleplay (45 min, via a "wheel": Speedball / War / Executive Takeover / Call Review / Heads
  Up), Thu = strategy training (top CSM trains the team), Fri = wrap-up + game tape (45-60 min). Daily
  agenda: Wins (start positive, leader shares too incl. mistakes) → Fires (5-10 min MAX, route them out
  — a media buyer fixes high CPL, not you) → weekly breakdown → board overview (verify DEFCON/last-TP/
  notes) → projections → daily agenda. Run a separate fulfillment call 2x/week so dept problems don't eat
  the dev meeting. The biggest management trap: "don't be their babysitter" — route them to
  resources/departments, don't solve it for them (A-players want to figure it out; doing it for them
  robs purpose). "How can I support you?" frame before "what the fuck is going on?" — default to an "us
  problem." Before firing, ask "have you actually shown them what success looks like?" — only then is it
  on them; PIP = 30 days. 1:1 triggers: scheduled, milestone, performance dip ~20% below baseline (call
  within 24-48h), behavioral red flag, emergency. EOD report (stress/energy 1-10, per-call self-audit,
  admin checklist, four-R KPIs daily) submitted same day — 3 strikes (warning/final/off-team). Manage to
  KPIs, not visible busywork (he killed a redundant admin doc when a CSM pushed back, but kept Monday +
  EODs + monthly projections as non-negotiables). Get a Head of CS only once you pass ~2-3 CSMs.
  → Lesson: CSM management — "3. Daily CSM meetings and framework" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: CSM management — "4. 1-1 Meetings with CSM's" · [COURSE-HIGH]
  → Lesson: CSM management — "6.CSM Eod report" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **Game tape / Five A's — kill the sales↔CS incongruency that churns clients.** Default failure mode:
  sales "throws the deal over the wall," CS has no idea what was promised; founders build that wall to
  keep cash flowing via overselling. Game tape tears it down. The Five A's: Arrange → Assemble → Assess
  (review the call against script/promises) → Analyze (each dept names ONE thing to do better) → Act
  (each dept head implements within 7 days). Weekly Friday by default; 3-5x/week if churn/misalignment
  is high. ~80% sales calls / 20% CS calls (most incongruity lives in sales; reviewing CS calls also
  feeds sales new selling points). External-accountability effect: when reps know calls get reviewed
  team-wide, they stop overselling. Mine side benefits: marketing pulls fresh hooks/pain points from
  the calls; fulfillment learns why clients buy. (Pay closers on cash collected and claw back commission
  on refunds to reduce overselling.) Resolve cross-role incentive conflicts (CSM bonused on churn wants
  to refund; call-center mgr earns on sets, wants to deny) by making each understand the other's comp +
  a 10-min alignment call.
  → Lesson: CSM management — "5. Game tape" · [COURSE-HIGH]

- **CSM replacement: panic → process; ejection vs planned transition; blame externally.** Trust your
  gut — sudden sucking-up / never any constructive criticism / "everything's fine" but never expands =
  churn/poaching risk (the poaching CSM "Cat" registered a competing domain while saying she was "super
  happy"; Cameron fired her with no plan → 33% churn, agency "slowly dying" 3-5 months). **Scenario 1
  (emergency ejection)**: off-board from everything via a single company-email password reset — but do
  NOT delete from Slack (shows "deactivated user" on all messages → client panic; just reset the
  password). Build a "Mission Save Churn" list, batch "founder-to-founder" calls over ~2 days (HARDEST
  clients first), don't mention the departure in invites, blame EXTERNALLY ("we have super-high
  standards; my job is to raise standards, not lower them"), reassure fulfillment improves. Turn hiring
  ads on full blast immediately (5x your normal hiring spend). **Scenario 2 (planned transition)**:
  30-45 day transition, optional ~$500 handoff bonus for a zero-churn handoff; the outgoing CSM tells
  clients they're "promoted / moving to a new strategic role" (NEVER "leaving/fired/hiring someone
  new"). Hand your own clients to a new CSM EASIEST-first (opposite of the save-churn order), with
  gradual 3-call authority transfer for attached clients (Call 1 you lead, Call 2 CSM runs stats, Call 3
  you show up 5-10 min "late" and let the CSM run the whole thing). Always onboard team on a COMPANY
  email (single-reset off-boarding; he leaked P&Ls because people had personal-email access).
  → Lesson: CSM management — "7.CSM Full replacement process" · [COURSE-HIGH]

## Decision rules
- If churn is climbing → audit the **launch call** first (what expectation did you fail to set), add a
  slide, then audit relationship cadence — and audit every churn (watch the exit call + the prior
  check-in; usually "they just didn't follow the process" and the CSM was afraid to call them out).
  Don't just chase better results.
- If a client is in the first 90 days or underperforming → tighter cadence + always BAMFAM; if stable/
  crushing it after 90 days → on-demand check-ins. NEVER go fully silent on stats (a 2-yr client churned
  "haven't made money in months" because he sent reports but stopped calling). Cadence is
  performance-weighted: of ~80 clients ~30% no-calls / ~50% biweekly / ~20% monthly.
- If a client is angry → get in the angry boat (over-own), let them fully vent, Acknowledge-&-Clarify per
  problem (inflate it until they pull back), ASK PERMISSION before your side; solutions before proof;
  assume the path forward — never ask "do you still want to continue?"
- If they demand to cancel/refund → call within the hour, book the Exit call as the company's #1 slot
  within 24h (contract-required); save them (Socratic dismantle + churn ladder) or harvest brutal
  feedback — but stop pushing once they've clearly said no multiple times.
- If they're disrespectful / scope-creeping / paying late → run a Reset call from a prepared boundary
  list: realign hard boundaries or part ways at month-end.
- If you/your team made a mistake → initiate an Ownership call BEFORE they catch it; take the hit in 30s,
  lead with Solution + Prevention; compensate only if warranted (from the back pocket).
- If an emotional client panics over one data point → maintain frame, let the monkey brain vent, reassure
  it's normal, refuse the irrational change, redirect to data.
- If a client wants a change to the proven ads/funnel that could hurt results → don't bend; "do you mind
  if I shoot it straight?" + authority stack (scale, 80/20, "you came for results not the prettiest
  thing"), then offer the clean choice: "(a) we do what you want knowing it could hurt results, or (b) we
  do what we KNOW works — which?" "The ugliest ads often outperform the sexiest."
- If a client says "the leads are bad" → don't change strategy on a feeling; check the stat sheet — no
  detailed notes / no financing applied = smokescreen, it's a sales issue (~80%); fully filled with valid
  declines = a real quality issue (~20%) → then add a landing-page qualifier.
- If a client just had a win or said something positive → ask for the review NOW (Zoom > phone > voice
  note > text), and stretch the yes into a full interview.
- If feedback contradicts itself → ignore single opinions; act only when 3-5+ clients name the same
  problem (a fire, not an opinion).
- If client management eats >20-30% of your week (or churn is rising) at ~10-15 clients with cash flow →
  hire a CSM at ~75-80% capacity; but only once you have ~5 gold-standard recordings. Don't take "I'm
  capped" at face value — shadow them a day and do the call math (his assumed cap moved 20→40→50→80).
- If you/your team don't know the answer on a call → never blow smoke; "I don't have all the info to be
  certain; I'll connect with the team and get back to you by [date]." BUT pair it with assurance that
  SOMEONE on the team will know (uncertainty in you = uncertainty in them).
- If a client churns → keep the bridge open (Lost Good vs Bad Terms), capture structured reasons, run the
  monthly churn-audit agent, nurture ~90 days for reactivation (warmest, ~$0-CAC lead you have).
- If a dispute arises about what was said/promised → resolve it with Fathom timestamps ("Ask Fathom"),
  not memory.
