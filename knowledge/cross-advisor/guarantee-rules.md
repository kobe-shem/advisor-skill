# Guarantee rules — what makes a guarantee real, and what makes it dumb

Binding doctrine for any offer this skill helps design. A guarantee is a risk-reversal device, not a
description of doing the job. Before proposing ANY guarantee, it must clear BOTH bars below. If it
can't clear both, do not bolt one on — de-risk through offer structure instead.

## Bar 1 — A real no-brainer for the BUYER (economic sense)
The buyer math has to be lopsidedly in their favor. Test: would the buyer say "I'd be stupid not to
take this"? Example that passes: "Pay $5k, we get you 15 jobs or we keep going until you do" — 15
jobs is worth ~$100k, so even if it's slow the math is obvious. The guarantee must make the downside
feel gone and the upside obvious.

## Bar 2 — No uncontrollable or uncapped liability for the SELLER
- Never guarantee an outcome you don't control. (A "15 jobs in 90 days" guarantee is a great SELLING
  device, and it burns you on delivery precisely because you don't control the outcome. Compelling to
  sell, painful to honor — that combination is the trap.)
- Never "work for free until you get the result" — it creates **uncapped delivery liability** for a
  service business. Prefer conditional or performance guarantees instead.

## The dumb-guarantee test (cut it if it fails)
A guarantee is DUMB and must be cut if it just restates "we'll deliver what we promised, or we'll keep
working until we do." That is not a risk reversal — it's a description of doing your job. It is
especially hollow when the client pays UPFRONT / one-time: "you stop paying until it works" means
nothing if they already paid, so it collapses to "we keep working free until it does the thing we
said." For a done-for-you build paid upfront, that kind of guarantee is noise. Cut it.

## Match the guarantee to the offer TYPE
- **Outcome / lead services** (marketing, lead gen, database reactivation) where the unit of value is
  high and the buyer math is obvious → a bold, measurable outcome guarantee can be a true no-brainer.
  Use it ONLY if the outcome is attributable AND the liability is capped/controllable.
- **Done-for-you builds paid upfront** (custom AI installs) → the risk reversal comes from STRUCTURE,
  not a bolted-on promise:
  - a paid, prescriptive diagnostic FIRST, so they see exactly what they get and the value case before
    the big check;
  - fixed scope / fixed price / fixed date (clarity is the reversal);
  - value-share pricing where attribution is clean (no value → no big payment; the pricing IS the
    reversal);
  - optionally an adoption guarantee ("we get your team live on it"), which is controllable and
    attacks the real failure mode (non-adoption).
  Do NOT bolt a "live by date or we keep working free" promise onto an upfront-paid build.

## Performance / value-share pricing needs clean attribution
A "% of value created" model (e.g. 5% of recovered/added profit) is a strong built-in risk reversal
ONLY where the value is directly measurable and attributable to the build (recovered/reactivated
revenue, recovered jobs). It breaks where value is subjective (efficiency, data cleanup, "enables
future scale") and there it invites disputes and refunds. Subjective-value work → fixed productized
price, not value-share.

## Default
When in doubt, no gimmick guarantee. De-risk through the structure: paid prescription → they see the
value case → fixed scope/price, or value-share on attributable upside. A prescription a buyer can see
beats a clever guarantee they don't believe.
