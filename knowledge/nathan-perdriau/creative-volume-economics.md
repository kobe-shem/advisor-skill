## Claims

NP-CVE-01. ROAS/MER targets set without gross margin cannot maximize profit; manage contribution profit = (revenue × gross margin) − ad spend ($10K revenue, 70% margin, $1K spend → $6K). An identical media plan (same revenue, spend, ROAS) 7×'s net profit at 70% margin and multiplies losses 2.5× at 40%.
— source: "Why You Need Gross Margin to Set ROAS Targets" @ 0:10 & 13:59

NP-CVE-02. Platform ROAS over-credits via brand search and warm retargeting; a 10–30 in-platform ROAS is almost never real (≈6–7 of 100 brands run ~10). Prefer MER (total revenue ÷ total marketing spend) over ROAS, CAC (actual orders) over attributed CPA, and new-customer MER for paid social.
— source: "Why You Need Gross Margin to Set ROAS Targets" @ 8:46

NP-CVE-03. Marginal returns at scale: rising CAC is the one fundamental truth of advertising — each added dollar buys colder audiences — so absolute contribution profit, not the ratio, decides budget raises. Worked: 8× spend at ROAS 6.25 vs 10 adds $21K/month contribution profit; 14× spend for 4× revenue still 4×'s profit; net profit 7×'s while ROAS falls 10 → 2.8. A lower ROAS at far higher spend makes far more money.
— source: "Why You Need Gross Margin to Set ROAS Targets" @ 2:37 & 7:36

NP-CVE-04. Creative-volume calculator: define a winner; measure average revenue per winner, run-length, and hit rate; compute launches/month for the revenue goal — layering in non-winner revenue and a returning-customer adjustment. Example: 11 more ads/month to grow $130K → $160K net sales.
— source: "Here's Exactly How Many Creatives You Need To Hit Revenue Targets" @ 7:44

NP-CVE-05. Per-ad economics: expected revenue per ad = mean spend per ad × new-customer MER; expected profit contribution per ad = that revenue − its spend. Normalize by active days: $2,000/ad over a 70-day lifespan means a $200K/month goal needs ~300 launches, not 100; past ~$0.5–1M/month spend, requirements go exponential (800–900 ads).
— source: "Creative Planning That Considers Finance (eCommerce)" @ 4:40 & 16:43

NP-CVE-06. ~95% of launched ads don't work, so production cost per ad must sit well below expected profit per ad — $400 expected profit → cap production near $150, roadmap sub-$100; production cost = creative-team comp ÷ ads shipped. A $15–20K shoot yielding 5–10 statics is built to lose; batch recording (10 hooks × 3 bodies × 2 CTAs = 60 ads from ~4.5 recorded minutes) collapses the cost.
— source: "Creative Planning That Considers Finance (eCommerce)" @ 3:05 & 20:26

NP-CVE-07. An "ad" must differ enough to be a statistically meaningful A/B unit — color, headline, primary-text, and landing-page changes are NOT new ads; a concept varies offer/angle/audience (what/why/who). Pin revenue definitions too (e.g. net sales + shipping collected); forecasts hold only when definitions match the data behind them.
— source: "Here's Exactly How Many Creatives You Need To Hit Revenue Targets" @ 3:25

NP-CVE-08. Creative volume is the most under-estimated bottleneck, but once fixed it stops binding: a $3–10M/year brand launching 20 ads/week usually needs 40–60, not 300. Scale volume only until the bottleneck moves elsewhere.
— source: "Creative Planning That Considers Finance (eCommerce)" @ 12:53

## Decision rules

- No gross margin on file (lead-gen: gross profit per revenue event) → no firm ROAS/CPL target; provisional + flagged.
- Judge budget raises on forecast incremental contribution profit dollars, never the ratio delta alone; show both.
- Platform ROAS ≥ ~8 → assume over-attribution; check MER before scaling.
- Re-run the calculator monthly with the account's own hit rate and winner active-days; only distinct creatives count toward quota.
- Cap production cost per shipped creative at ~1/3 of expected contribution; if required volume exceeds capacity, report the revenue target as unreachable.

## Teaching nuggets

- "Would you take 14× the spend for 4× the revenue?" Sounds awful as a ratio — it 4×'s profit. The ratio is a fraction; the bank account is a number. (For budget-raise conversations.)
- Efficiency falls as spend grows because each extra dollar buys a colder stranger — physics, not failure. The test: does the next dollar still return over a dollar of contribution profit?
- The same dashboard is a rocket at 70% margin, bankruptcy at 40%.
