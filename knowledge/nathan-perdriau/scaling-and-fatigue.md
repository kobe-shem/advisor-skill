## Claims

NP-SF-01. Portfolio model: 3 ads jointly holding $1K/day at a blended 4x beats 1 ad holding it all — any one can fatigue and the rest absorb. Brands built on ~3 hero ads end up ~40% down year-on-year when those fatigue; the fix had to start 6–8 months earlier.
— source: "Meta Ads Creative Strategy in 2026: The Full System" @ 2:56–5:12

NP-SF-02. Every ad has a fixed total lifetime spend capacity. Pushing daily spend pulls fatigue forward — the same ad can die in ~6 weeks pushed hard or live ~6 months at ~$100/day. Choose squeeze-vs-breathe from business constraints (inventory/cash), not return alone.
— source: same video @ 1:55:09–1:58:20

NP-SF-03. Two fatigue drivers: (1) staleness — max capacity reached or too similar to other live creatives (bundled, same audience); (2) audience too small — a niche callout has a hard daily-spend ceiling; scaling past it pushes delivery to colder, never-bullseye users, degrading CPA structurally.
— source: same video @ 1:58:24–2:01:30; "The Only Video You Need On Meta Ads In 2026" @ 11:00–12:15

NP-SF-04. Cold-ad frequency above ~2 is a red flag. Meta research he cites: after two impressions of an IDENTICAL creative, purchase probability declines precipitously; a novel creative as the 3rd/4th impression keeps it high — rotation, not repetition, sustains conversion.
— source: "Meta Ads Creative Strategy in 2026" @ 14:48–16:30

NP-SF-05. Never touch what's working: scale a KPI-hitting ad set by raising budget in place (+20%/day); don't relaunch winners elsewhere or inject new ads into a winning ad set (resets sequencing) — new creatives launch as a sibling ad set. A scaling campaign (duplicate the maxed winner's post ID, keep the original live) buys only ~+20% extra daily spend on a good day: pointless at $60/day (+$10), worth it at $400/day or for 10 winners at once (~+$1K/day). Never test inside scaling; KPI it at campaign level.
— source: "The Only Video You Need On Meta Ads In 2026" @ 44:57–45:16, 47:48–49:01, 52:25–53:05

NP-SF-06. Verdict spend: 3× target CPA before keep/kill (3+ conversions keep, 2 extend, 0–1 cut); he prefers 5× when affordable. Run tests ~1 week (3–4 days big/stable; 10–14 small or long-cycle) to absorb weekday seasonality and time-to-purchase. Daily test budget = target conversions × expected CPA ÷ days (20×$100÷14 ≈ $143/day); Meta's 50-in-7 learning rule blows out (~$714/day) — ignore it for sizing.
— source: same video @ 53:59–55:00, 57:19–57:38, 1:34:47–1:36:30

NP-SF-07. Fatigue rescue ladder: (1) rotate fresh hooks onto the fatigued hero — case: ads at ~$50–100K lifetime spend took ~50 new hooks (~1 hour to shoot) and spent another ~$100K at the same efficiency; (2) move the post ID into a cost-cap/scaling campaign — more spend, faster burn; (3) translate the winner into another format (video→static). Past that, the bench is the plan; don't duplicate ad sets.
— source: "Meta Ads Creative Strategy in 2026" @ 1:04:04–1:04:47, 2:01:42–2:03:25

NP-SF-08. Budget automation follows Pareto: CBO pushes 80% of spend into 20% of ads, compounding to ~4% of ads holding ~64% — maximum efficiency, maximum fragility. Use ABO to force spend across tests and diversify the portfolio; flip to CBO only for consciously-accepted short-term efficiency spikes.
— source: "Meta Ads Creative Strategy in 2026" @ 1:35:20–1:38:05

## Decision rules

- One ad >~50% of conversions → standing risk; accelerate successors (matches core portfolio rule).
- Cold-ad frequency >2 → saturation signal even if CPA holds.
- CPA degrades on every budget push → revert to last stable spend, add winners; a scaling layer only when ~+20% extra spend justifies the complexity.
- Never add ads to a KPI-hitting ad set; launch a sibling ad set.
- Hero fatigues → hooks first, format translation second, media-buying squeezes last; never duplicate ad sets.
- Test budget = conversions × CPA ÷ days; ignore the 50-in-7 rule for sizing.
- Retargeting sized by frequency (budget = audience × CPM × desired frequency, ≤~7/30d), judged on incrementality — never percent-of-spend (Only Video @ 1:31:09).

## Teaching nuggets

- A winning ad is a battery with fixed total charge: drain fast, dies in weeks; drain slow, lasts months. You pick the drain rate, not the capacity.
- Fatigue isn't the ad getting worse — it's the audience finishing it. Third identical impression ≈ near-zero new buying.
- Scaling means paying to reach colder people; CPA rises because the new viewers were never the bullseye.
- One mega-winner is a cliff, not a moat.
