## Claims

NP-CS-01. Andromeda reads each ad's transcript, copy, and cast to pick its audience; near-duplicate creatives get BUNDLED and served to one pool — frequency up, reach down, return down. Concept-diverse ads open separate audiences and unlock spend.
— source: "Meta Ads Creative Strategy in 2026: The Full System" @ 9:44–14:08

NP-CS-02. Concept = persona × angle × offer (format is a weaker 4th lever); diversity requires changing at least one. One concept per ad set, stacking 20–100+ creatives under it; mixed concepts in one ad set send conflicting targeting signals.
— source: same video @ 26:04–27:20, 43:42–45:28; Instagram reel DWvXJrxkweZ

NP-CS-03. Testing priority: angle first, offer second, persona third, format last. If a persona that has bought before stops converting, it's an angle/offer problem; no format "works" as a concept — the script inside it does.
— source: same video @ 38:20–43:40

NP-CS-04. Personas are problem archetypes, not demographics; broad personas ("women 25–45") produce forgettable creative. Build 30–40 specific ones manually before letting an LLM extend the list, then curate hard — raw LLM personas regress to the mean.
— source: same video @ 29:21–33:14

NP-CS-05. The hook is a relevance promise, not a trick; ~80% of viewers never watch past it. Grade hooks on clarity, relevance, novelty, specificity, credibility — a couple done well suffices. Problem agitation beats explicit persona callouts: people skip "X owners, listen up" but watch their named pain.
— source: same video @ 48:53–53:11

NP-CS-06. Given good body scripting, 2 bodies × 15 hooks beats 5 bodies × 2 hooks — the performance delta is larger in the hook. A hook has three layers (visual, audio, overlay copy); a new hook gets its own creative ID and reaches a novel audience.
— source: same video @ 49:24–49:44, 1:03:14–1:04:47

NP-CS-07. Bridges (hook→body, body→CTA) ruin otherwise-good ads: pitching the product right after the hook collapses conversion. Introduce the product as late as possible, walking viewers unaware→problem-aware→solution-aware→product-aware; later intro = fewer viewers, more total conversions.
— source: same video @ 59:05–1:03:10

NP-CS-08. Test a concept with 10+ ads; at typical ~5% ad hit rates, ~20 ads to expect one winner. Most ads cap at ~$700–$1.5K mean lifetime spend, so volume must compensate; 1–3 executions is under-testing.
— source: same video @ 25:31–25:50, 1:27:18–1:27:47

NP-CS-09. Volume math: ~1 new ad per $1,000 monthly spend (AUD ≈ USD roughly); better: expected revenue per ad = mean spend per ad × average return, then divide the new-customer revenue target by it. Production budget: founder-led under ~$30K/mo, ~25% of media budget at $30–100K/mo, ~10% above $100K/mo.
— source: same video @ 1:44:31–1:48:50

NP-CS-10. Nobody — including experts — can pick which good ad wins; the skill is defining the 7/10 quality bar and maximizing volume above it. 2,000+ ads/month below the bar is activity, not strategy.
— source: same video @ 5:12–6:15, 1:41:33–1:44:14

## Decision rules

- Brief = persona × angle × offer BEFORE format; demographic-shaped personas go back for sharpening.
- Proven persona underperforming → rotate angle, then offer; persona/format last.
- Hook rate below account average → swap hooks before judging the body.
- Script pitches product immediately after hook → rewrite the bridge.
- New concept → plan ≥10 executions; never call it dead at 1–3 ads.
- Volume ≈ 1 new ad / $1K monthly spend, or revenue target ÷ expected value per ad.
- Cheap pre-test: post drafts as trial reels, CTA cut; use retention graphs to cull hooks before paid spend (tactic dated ~April 2026).

## Teaching nuggets

- Meta picks who sees your ad by reading the ad itself. Ten lookalike ads just shout at the same people more often.
- A concept is one sentence: WHO × WHY-they-care × WHAT we offer. New footage without changing one of those is not a new test.
- Most viewers leave in 3 seconds; the first 3 seconds buys all the rest. Make more openings, not more middles.
- Don't guess the winner — nobody can. Make every ad good enough to deserve a test, then let spend decide.
