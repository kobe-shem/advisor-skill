## Claims

NP-GD-01. Max month-over-month growth is a cash-cycle number, not an ad number: contribution margin after product costs, sell-through rate, OPEX, and marketing efficiency set the working capital generated — the worked example caps at 4.7%/month. Profitable ≠ growable: a business netting $15–16k/month can sit at 0% max growth — cash leaves before the P&L records the sale.
— source: "How To Calculate Your Maximum Growth Rate % (Free Calculator)" @ 6:45, 12:56 (78L91ArNkag)

NP-GD-02. If target growth > max growth, three moves exist: improve P&L inputs, lower the target, or borrow — leverage only works while profit covers repayments (6% of $400k = $24k/month, eating most of ~$35k profit). Max-rate growth also means $0 dividends — make the owner choose: draws now, or growth for an exit.
— source: same @ 7:20, 15:14, 17:39

NP-GD-03. Profit levers ranked: (1) units per transaction, (2) discount rate, (3) cost of goods/delivery, (4) CAC — a $5 price bump moved profit contribution $16→$28/order; ~5–10% gains on every line at once compounded $16→$34, doubling profit. Contribution margin after CAC caps net profit when repeat revenue is sub ~15% of revenue (12.61% − ~10% OPEX = 2% max net). Shifting creative/budget to highest-gross-margin top-decile products cuts average COGS with no renegotiation ($5 lower = +33% profit).
— source: "How to Double Your Profit Using Levers in Your Unit Economics" @ 13:37, 14:05, 21:48, 7:05, 20:45 (dQymzycGdPE)

NP-GD-04. A heavy-discounting spiral (22% give-aways on acquisition) was root-caused not to weak demand but to low creative velocity and account-structure issues — creative volume generates demand without surrendering margin.
— source: same @ 17:04

NP-GD-05. 7- vs 8-figure separators, in order: product (market fit, unit economics, portfolio depth), distribution (low acquisition cost + channel diversity), talent (revenue capacity ≈ sum of team skills), risk appetite. Headroom to spend ≥25% of revenue on marketing is required to reach 8 figures on paid. Channel diversity also creates a halo on paid: near-identical accounts can show 10 vs 4 in-platform ROAS where the only difference is investment elsewhere (SEO after 3–4 years, activations, collabs).
— source: "Here's What Seperate's 7 & 8 Figure eCommerce Businesses" @ 2:30, 5:16, 10:21, 15:54, 8:21 (vcIqua9xKys)

NP-GD-06. KOOKAÏ Australia (9-figure fashion brand): ROAS "looked good, almost too good" — platforms claim existing demand. Fix: creative velocity (+120% volume, ramped only after per-ad profit contribution beat production cost), account structure (existing customers + site visitors excluded from cold, 7-day-click attribution, branded terms out of non-brand search), geo-lift incrementality over attribution. Results: AU Google YoY growth 119%→362%, Meta ~0%→327%, US new customers +645% in 30 days, Spain launch at 5.6 incremental ROI.
— source: "How We Accelerated KOOKAÏ Australia's Growth Rate By 100%" @ 0:34, 4:56, 6:40, 3:13, 11:40 (ZzLLUVmlTXE)

NP-GD-07. Paid media acquires; it does not retain: repeated incrementality tests on existing-customer lists found zero incremental conversions; 1-day-view attribution trains the optimizer toward existing customers.
— source: same @ 5:31

## Decision rules

- If target growth > max cash-cycle growth, present the three-option fork (NP-GD-02) — never pace to an unfundable target.
- If ads perform but spend can't scale, report the binding constraint (payback, ops capacity, cash) — don't force budget.
- Profit complaints: audit all four levers before promising a lower CPL (NP-GD-03); offers sweetened to buy volume → check creative velocity first (NP-GD-04).
- Platform numbers too good → demand incrementality evidence (NP-GD-06); exclude existing customers from cold campaigns and prefer 7-day-click (NP-GD-07).

## Teaching nuggets

- Growth eats cash before revenue lands — the P&L can show profit while the bank account shrinks.
- The fastest profit doubling is rarely inside the ad account: small simultaneous improvements at every funnel line compound at the bottom.
- Bottleneck-finding is the operator's core skill: product economics, distribution, team capacity, or risk appetite — one is always the limiter.
