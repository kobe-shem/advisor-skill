# Offers — $100M Offers (distilled)

## The Grand Slam Offer
An offer so good the prospect stops asking "how much?" and starts asking "how do I start?"
Attention shifts entirely from price to outcome. Four components have to fire together:
1. A targeted market with a desperate problem (a starving crowd).
2. A dream outcome they crave.
3. A vehicle that delivers it with low effort and fast results.
4. Risk reversal that removes the downside.

The #1 failure mode is selling a commodity and competing on price. The fix is always to
differentiate so hard that direct comparison becomes impossible.

## The Value Equation — the most important formula
```
          Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
VALUE = ───────────────────────────────────────────────────────
                 Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice
```
Two levers raise value (numerator), two lower it (denominator):
- **Dream Outcome** ↑ — make the destination vivid and specific. Sell the result, not the mechanism.
- **Perceived Likelihood** ↑ — proof: case studies, testimonials, track record, specificity. "Will it work for *me*?"
- **Time Delay** ↓ — compress time to first win; engineer an early quick win.
- **Effort & Sacrifice** ↓ — the less they must do, the higher the value. Done-for-you > done-with-you > DIY.

**Key insight:** most people compete by adding *more* (modules, calls, content) — that's competing
on features. The real, under-used levers are perceived likelihood (proof) and time delay (speed).

## Market selection — the starving crowd
Priority order: **Starving Crowd > Offer Strength > Sales Skill.** A mediocre offer wins in a
desperate market; a perfect offer dies in a dead one. Three criteria for a great market:
1. Massive, active pain.
2. Purchasing power.
3. Reachable / easy to target consistently.

The four evergreen markets: **health, wealth, relationships, happiness.**

**Niching down raises price** because it concentrates pain and willingness to pay.
"Time management" ($) → "time management for sales reps" ($$) → "time management for B2B SaaS AEs
closing $500k+ deals" ($$$). Nail one niche, then expand horizontally.

## Pricing — charge to value, not to cost
- Charge as much as you can say out loud without flinching.
- Never compete on price — someone will always be willing to go broke beating you.
- High price signals quality and screens for better clients; the hardest price-negotiators are the worst clients.
- Price on value delivered, never on hours invested.
- If no one ever pushes back on price, it is too low.
- **FTE anchor:** position the offer against the fully-loaded cost of a full-time employee doing
  the same job (salary + benefits + management + mistakes + churn). It reframes the price from a
  cost into a replacement.

## The offer-creation process
1. **Identify the dream outcome** — the real destination, specific and measurable.
2. **List every obstacle** between them and it — exhaustively.
3. **Turn each obstacle into a solution** — "what would remove this?" Each becomes a deliverable.
4. **Trim** to deliverables that are high value to them, low cost to you.
5. **Name each deliverable for its outcome,** not its mechanism.
6. **Stack** every element with its standalone value so the price looks small against the total.

## Bonuses — strategic objection removers
Bonuses are not extras; each one should kill a specific objection (if it doesn't, cut it).
- Name every bonus separately with a stated perceived value.
- Stack in ascending value — strongest bonus last.
- The stack should feel worth more than the core.
- Types: **speed** bonus (kills time objection), **proof** bonus (kills skepticism),
  **implementation/done-for-you** bonus (kills effort objection), **insurance** bonus (kills risk).

## Guarantees — the four types (risk reversal)
Risk is the enemy of buying. The guarantee answers "what if it doesn't work?" before it's asked.
1. **Unconditional** (strongest, simplest) — money-back, no questions. Use with high product confidence / low refund risk.
2. **Conditional** — refund only if they did the work and still didn't get the result. Screens for serious buyers, reduces refunds.
3. **Anti-guarantee** (premium signal) — no refunds; we only take committed clients. Use for high fulfillment cost / bespoke / ultra-premium; filters tire-kickers.
4. **Performance** (B2B / high ticket) — pay tied to a measurable result / rev-share / profit-share. Use only where attribution is clean. Maximum alignment.

Stack unconditional + conditional for the strongest combined reversal.

> **Caution — uncapped liability:** some Hormozi material also lists a "we work for free until you
> get the result" guarantee. Treat it as a last resort: it creates uncapped delivery liability for
> a service business. Prefer a conditional or performance guarantee, which cap the downside.

## Scarcity & urgency — ethical pressure only
- **Scarcity (supply):** "we cap clients per cohort to protect results." Must be real.
- **Urgency (time):** real deadlines, real price increases. Resetting timers / fake scarcity destroys trust permanently.
- Engineer *real* constraints by design: cap load, run cohorts, raise price every N clients.

## Naming
Name the offer and each deliverable for the **outcome**, not the format. Pattern:
`[Specific Outcome] for [Specific Audience] in [Timeframe] without [Objection]`.

## Decision rules
- If close rate is low, fix the **offer** before the sales script — most "sales problems" are offer problems.
- If you're tempted to add a feature, add **proof or speed** instead (the levers buyers actually price).
- If buyers haggle on price, the offer isn't differentiated enough — don't discount, increase perceived value or change scope.
- If the market isn't starving, no offer craft will save it — change the market first.
- Choose a guarantee by fulfillment risk: low risk → unconditional; need-qualified buyers → conditional; bespoke/premium → anti-guarantee; clean attribution + high ticket → performance.
