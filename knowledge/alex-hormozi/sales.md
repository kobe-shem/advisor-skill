# Sales — CLOSER & objection handling (distilled)

## The CLOSER call framework
A sales call runs in order; each step has one job.

- **C — Clarify.** Establish why you're both here and what they're actually trying to solve.
  Confirm a real problem and real motivation exist. "What specifically made you reach out today?"
- **L — Label.** Repeat the problem back precisely, in their own language, with its cost.
  Makes them feel understood; builds instant trust. "So it's [pain], and that's costing you [consequence] — right?"
- **O — Overview past pain.** Ask what they've already tried and why it failed. Surfaces
  objections early and positions your solution as categorically different.
- **S — Sell the vacation.** Sell the destination, not the process. Put them 90 days in the
  future with the outcome already real, and ask what that changes for them. Build emotional
  buy-in to the future state *before* price.
- **E — Explain the offer.** Present the full stacked offer, each component with its value, then
  the price — so the price feels small against the total laid out.
- **R — Reinforce.** After the yes, reinforce the decision and lay out exactly what happens next
  and the first win's timeline. Reduces buyer's remorse, locks commitment.

## The AAA objection framework — Acknowledge → Associate → Ask
Never fight an objection. Validate it, connect it to a solved case, then ask for the decision.
- **Acknowledge:** "That's a fair concern" — never dismiss or argue.
- **Associate:** connect to a past client who had the same worry and the outcome they got.
- **Ask:** a confident, direct close — "Given that, does it make sense to move forward?"

**Common objections (paraphrased moves):**
- *"I need to think about it"* → ask what specifically: the investment, the timing, or whether it'll work — then help them think it through now.
- *"It's too expensive"* → separate "don't have the money" from "not sure it's worth it." If worth, anchor the cost of *not* solving it against the price and the payback window.
- *"I need to talk to my partner"* → ask what the partner will be most concerned about, and arm them to answer it now rather than later.

## Price framing
- Sell the **outcome**, not the process or feature list.
- Anchor price against the cost of the unsolved problem and (for B2B) the fully-loaded cost of an FTE doing the same job.
- Negotiate **scope, never price** — discounting trains the worst-fit buyers and signals low value.

## Sales-team principles
- Track close rate of *qualified* calls, and the top 3 recurring objections — those reveal offer/positioning gaps, not just rep gaps.
- A new rep added to a proven team will underperform the existing reps for months; plan for that capacity/conversion dip when scaling sales (see `scaling-constraints.md`).
- The best clients are the ones you can get the best results for — not the easiest to close.

## Decision rules
- If close rate on qualified calls is low and the script is sound, the problem is upstream — fix the offer (`offers.md`) or the lead quality (`leads.md`) before retraining reps.
- If reps are arguing with objections, switch them to AAA — acknowledge first, never rebut.
- If a prospect stalls, isolate the *real* objection with one direct question before answering anything.
- Always sell the vacation before the price; presenting price before the future-state is built kills the close.
